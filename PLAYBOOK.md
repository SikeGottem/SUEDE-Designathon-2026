# SUEDE Designathon 2026 — winning playbook

Prepared 1 September 2026 for Ethan, Darius, CC, Akari, and Chloe.

## The call

You are not preparing to “do a designathon.” You are preparing to win a short interaction-design competition against roughly 30 teams by being clearer, narrower, better validated, and better rehearsed than everyone else.

The winning shape is:

> one recognisable user + one painful moment + one memorable intervention + one tested hero loop + one sharp story

Do not build a broad platform. Do not divide the work into five disconnected mini-projects. Do not wait until Sunday to begin the pitch.

## Why this strategy is evidence-backed

### 2024 rubric

SUEDE publicly scored five areas in 2024:

1. Visual communication
2. Problem identification
3. Solution approach
4. Design innovation
5. Presentation skills

Until the 2026 rubric appears, use those five areas as an equal-weight internal scorecard. They cover both substance and communication, and they align with the winning work visible in later years.

### 2025 field and winners

The 2025 edition had 135 Devpost participants and public accounts describe roughly 150 students / 30 teams. The brief asked teams to make critical and mindful digital engagement effortless in routine contexts.

- **1st — Hot Topic:** reworked the comment area already underneath short-form video. It focused on one behaviour, used prompts/cards/polls to turn passive scrolling into a meaningful conversation, and presented an integrated interaction rather than another destination app.
- **2nd — LLAMALERT:** used a memorable llama interaction to flag misinformation, explain why, and turn learning into a small game. The concept was instantly retellable.
- **3rd — TOUCAN:** used a lightweight overlay around a moment of need. The team deliberately narrowed to learning, awareness, and convenience.
- **People’s Choice — Algo:** made an algorithmic bubble legible, like a “Spotify Wrapped” for a feed, then added small daily nudges. Its creators specifically credited storytelling and research.

The useful inference is not “make another social app.” It is that SUEDE rewards a precise intervention in an existing behaviour, visible user logic, memorable branding, and a strong demonstration.

## Decision system

### Five-part scorecard

Score every concept 1–5 on each dimension. A concept must reach **20/25** before high-fidelity design.

| Dimension | The question |
|---|---|
| Problem | Is the user and painful moment specific, important, and evidenced? |
| Effectiveness | Does the core interaction directly change the target behaviour? |
| Innovation | Is the mechanism distinct and memorable, without relying on novelty theatre? |
| Craft | Can the hero loop look and feel excellent within the available time? |
| Story | Can a judge understand and repeat the idea after one sentence and one demo? |

### Non-negotiable gates

- **Gate 1 — concept lock:** no later than 90 minutes after the brief is released.
- **Gate 2 — evidence lock:** do not start full high-fi until at least three real user conversations/tests contradict or support the core assumption.
- **Gate 3 — hero-loop lock:** the main trigger → action → payoff path works before visual expansion.
- **Gate 4 — feature freeze:** by Saturday 5pm. After that, only clarity, polish, testing, accessibility, pitch, and submission work.
- **Gate 5 — submission-safe:** a complete submittable version exists at least 90 minutes before the official deadline.

If the team cannot agree, Ethan chooses after hearing one 60-second case from each side. No decision gets more than 12 minutes unless it risks disqualification.

## Research and testing protocol

### Ask for behaviour, not opinions

Bad: “Would you use this?”

Good:

- “Tell me about the last time this happened.”
- “What did you do next?”
- “Show me where you would tap first.”
- “What do you think will happen after this?”
- “What would stop you from trusting this?”

Capture exact quotes, observed behaviour, and failed assumptions. Never invent user evidence. A small number of real observations beats a decorative survey chart.

### Minimum proof package

- 5–8 conversations or usability sessions
- one specific primary user, not “everyone”
- one observed current workaround
- one core insight that changed the design
- before/after evidence from at least one iteration
- one meaningful success measure
- one feasibility/trust note

## Mentor protocol

Use mentors as judges-in-training, not as outsourced ideators. Bring a decision and a targeted question.

1. **Concept gate:** “Which assumption in our problem–solution logic is weakest?”
2. **Prototype gate:** “Where does this interaction feel confusing, fake, or untrustworthy?”
3. **Pitch gate:** “What would stop this from advancing, and what single proof would change that?”

After every session, record: advice, whether the team accepts it, the reason, owner, and deadline. Judges notice visible learning; they do not reward mentor name-dropping.

## The prototype

Build 5–7 excellent frames around one hero loop:

1. Context / trigger
2. Entry point
3. Core action
4. System response
5. User decision
6. Meaningful payoff
7. One failure, trust, privacy, or accessibility state

Everything else is optional. The prototype must demonstrate why the mechanism works, not simulate an entire company.

Accessibility is part of effectiveness: readable type, sufficient contrast, obvious focus/selection, alternatives to colour-only meaning, and plain-language microcopy.

If AI is involved, explain the input, output, uncertainty, user control, failure mode, and why AI is necessary. “Powered by AI” without trust logic is a liability.

## The pitch

Until the official pitch duration is confirmed, rehearse both a 3-minute and a 5-minute version.

### Three-minute spine

| Time | Job |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:20 | Put the judge inside one human moment |
| 0:20–0:45 | Show the evidence and sharp problem statement |
| 0:45–1:00 | Deliver the one-line product and memorable mechanism |
| 1:00–1:50 | Demo the hero loop; no feature tour |
| 1:50–2:15 | Show what testing changed and why users trust it |
| 2:15–2:40 | Explain impact, feasibility, accessibility, and next step |
| 2:40–3:00 | Return to the opening human and land one sentence |

One lead speaker is clearer than five forced speaking parts. Other members own demo control and Q&A areas. Keep a screen-recorded backup on two devices.

### Likely Q&A attacks

- Why this user and not everyone?
- What did you learn from real people?
- Why will behaviour actually change?
- Why does this need to exist inside/outside the current platform?
- What is novel beyond visual styling?
- What happens when the system is wrong?
- What is the simplest path to implementation?
- How is it accessible and safe?
- What did you deliberately not build?

## Kill list

- No solution work before the official start unless organisers explicitly permit it.
- No “app for everyone.”
- No more than three concepts after the first hour.
- No high-fi before basic evidence and low-fi testing.
- No feature added after Saturday 5pm.
- No survey used as a substitute for behavioural research.
- No fake metrics, quotes, or technical claims.
- No live demo without a recording backup.
- No five-person committee decisions on every pixel.
- No all-nighter as a badge of honour.
- No pitch that spends more time on process than the user’s changed outcome.

## Win conditions

By final submission, the team should be able to answer yes to all of these:

- Can a judge repeat the idea after hearing one sentence?
- Is the user and moment specific?
- Did real evidence materially change the design?
- Does the prototype prove one complete interaction?
- Is the mechanism distinctive without feature bloat?
- Is the visual craft consistent and purposeful?
- Are trust, accessibility, and feasibility credible?
- Is the pitch timed, rehearsed, and backed up?
- Was the submission safely completed before the deadline?

If any answer is no, fix that before adding another feature.
