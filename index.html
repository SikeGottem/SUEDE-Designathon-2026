The last published rubric had five parts.

Problem identification

Did the team identify and understand the problem?

Solution approach

Does the product effectively solve that problem?

Design innovation

Is the solution creative and innovative?

Visual communication

How well does the design look and feel?

Presentation skills

How clearly does the team communicate its ideas?

Confirmed SUEDE 2024 criteria. Reordered here to follow the logic of judging.

Problem identification.

2024 wordingHow well did you identify and understand the problem?
Who?Is the user specific enough to understand their needs?
When?Is there a clear moment where the problem occurs?
Why?Is the underlying cause explained, not only the symptom?
Evidence?What proves the problem matters to a real person?
Strong

One user, one situation, a clear tension and evidence that changes the framing.

Weak

A broad social issue, an invented persona and research facts with no consequence.

Solution approach.

2024 wordingHow effective is your product at solving the problem?
Connection?Does the mechanism directly address the cause you identified?
Outcome?What becomes easier, safer or better for the user?
Core flow?Can judges see the value happen in the prototype?
Scope?Is the main solution complete enough to believe?
Strong

A visible line from problem to action to outcome, shown through one complete flow.

Weak

A list of features that sounds useful but does not resolve the identified problem.

Design innovation.

2024 wordingHow creative and innovative is your solution?
Angle?Is the interpretation of the brief different from the obvious response?
Mechanism?Does it change behaviour in a useful or unexpected way?
Context?Is the idea placed where the problem actually happens?
Comparison?Can the team explain how it differs from existing solutions?
Strong

A distinct insight or mechanism with a clear reason it is better for this problem.

Weak

A familiar app, dashboard or chatbot with the brief added as content.

Visual communication.

2024 wordingHow well does your design look and feel?
Hierarchy?Can a judge see what matters and what happens next?
Consistency?Do type, colour, spacing and components feel deliberate?
Usability?Does the visual design help people understand the interaction?
Fit?Does the style suit the user, setting and idea?
Strong

Enough fidelity to communicate clearly, with craft focused on the critical moments.

Weak

Decoration, effects and perfect mockups covering an unclear interaction.

Presentation skills.

2024 wordingHow well does your team present and communicate your ideas?
Clarity?Can judges explain the idea back after hearing it once?
Story?Does the pitch move from problem to insight to solution to proof?
Demo?Is the important interaction shown without confusion?
Editing?Has the team removed details that do not support the argument?
Strong

One sentence for the idea, one clear demo and only the evidence needed to believe it.

Weak

A chronological process diary, too many speakers or a demo that needs narration to work.

The criteria are connected.

ProblemDo we understand it?
ApproachDoes the response make sense?
InnovationIs the angle distinct?
VisualsCan we see it clearly?
PitchCan we understand and remember it?

A polished final stage cannot fully repair a weak first stage.

What likely separates the overall winner.

A specific interpretation.

The project does not simply answer the topic. It finds a precise behaviour, setting or tension that other teams miss.

Visible impact.

The judge can see what changes for the user.

A believable flow.

The main interaction works as an argument.

Evidence of judgment.

Research and testing changed the design.

A memorable story.

The team makes the value easy to repeat.

Inference from the published 2024 rubric, 2025 winner language and judge reflections. Not an official 2026 weighting.

What Hot Topic shows.

The 2025 winner focused on the comments below videos people already watch. It addressed digital literacy inside an existing behaviour.

Why that is judge-friendly: the problem is specific, the angle is distinct and the core value is easy to demonstrate.

What FreTo shows.

The team compared four problems, gathered primary research, tested before high-fidelity design and documented what changed.

Why that is judge-friendly: it creates visible proof for problem identification, solution approach and design judgment.

What strong evidence looks like.

We understand the user.A sourced behaviour, direct observation or interview finding tied to a specific moment.
The problem matters.A visible consequence for the user, not only a large statistic.
The idea is useful.People can explain the value back or choose it over a clear alternative.
The flow works.A task test reveals where people succeed, hesitate or fail, followed by a design change.
The idea is distinct.A comparison with existing responses showing the different insight or mechanism.
The idea is feasible.A simple explanation of how it works, what is assumed and what is outside scope.

What judges can reject quickly.

It misses the brief.

The team solves a related issue but not the actual task.

It takes too long to understand.

The problem, user or value remains unclear after the opening explanation.

It feels generic.

The same idea could answer several unrelated briefs.

The prototype proves nothing.

Screens exist, but the important change for the user is not demonstrated.

The research is decorative.

Insights appear in the deck but do not affect the solution.

The submission is incomplete.

Required links, credits, files or team information are missing.

Review the concept like a judge.

What exact problem are we solving?
What evidence makes us believe it matters?
Why does this mechanism solve that problem?
What is different from the obvious response?
What can judges see working?
Can someone explain the idea back in one sentence?

Review the final entry like a judge.

Does every requirement appear in the submission?
Does the opening make the problem specific?
Does the demo show the core value clearly?
Do we show what research or testing changed?
Does the visual design help understanding?
What will judges remember ten minutes later?

What this means for our time.

Opening

Problem + innovation

Spend enough time to find a specific problem and a non-obvious response.

Middle

Approach + proof

Use the prototype and testing to show that the core mechanism works.

Final

Visuals + presentation

Make the strongest argument easy to see, understand and remember.

The schedule should follow the weakest part of the judging case, not a fixed production ritual.
