# SUEDE design authenticity

This gate catches work that is polished but interchangeable. It complements `suede-judge-review`; it does not replace the judging model or the declared visual lead.

## Read first

1. Read the relevant brief, evidence and settled decisions.
2. Read `WIKI/FRETO_REFERENCE.md` when FreTo is being used as a precedent.
3. Read the artifact's visual contract or write the compact contract below.
4. For a post-build review, inspect the actual screenshot, Figma frame, slide export or running interface at delivery size.

## Compact visual contract

Before a material visual build, state:

- **Communication job:** what must become clearer, more believable or more memorable.
- **Specific source anchors:** two to four real details from the subject, audience, evidence or environment that can shape the visual language.
- **Reference delta:** what is being borrowed from a reference and what must remain visibly different.
- **Rejected defaults:** at least three likely model defaults that will not be used, with a reason for each.
- **Signature move:** one visual or interaction decision whose meaning depends on this subject.
- **Restraint:** one attractive but unnecessary device that will be removed.
- **Human decision:** the user feedback, team choice, manual edit or tested finding that controls the direction.

Do not use mood adjectives alone as a contract. "Bold, clean and modern" makes no decision.

## Diverge in structure before styling

Create at least three materially different thumbnail compositions before polishing. They must change hierarchy, pacing or the way the evidence is understood, not merely swap colour and type.

For each direction, name:

- what the viewer notices first
- what becomes easier to understand
- the main risk
- which source anchor it expresses

Use one declared visual lead to execute the selected direction. Other broad visual skills may critique the render, but must not inject a second visual constitution during the same build.

An exploratory artifact may proceed without final human selection only when it is clearly labelled exploratory. It cannot become the deck or product source of truth until a human chooses, rejects or materially redirects it.

## AI-tell rejection scan

Treat these as warnings, not universal style bans. A pattern is acceptable only when the content or interaction genuinely requires it.

### Composition

- centered headline plus decorative background with no subject-specific visual
- equal cards, bento tiles or feature grids used because they are easy to generate
- a large coloured side rail plus repeated rows that could hold any content
- every idea enclosed in a rounded container
- perfectly repeated spacing and scale with no editorial rhythm or intentional exception
- a layout copied from a winner or reference without a stated reference delta

### Copy and labels

- stacked uppercase eyebrows, category tags and micro-metadata
- slogan fragments such as "One signal", "Built different" or three adjective claims
- process labels that narrate what the layout already shows
- vague design language such as seamless, elevate, unlock, reimagine or human-centred
- fake quotes, invented evidence, decorative metrics or unsupported precision
- clever microcopy that the team would not naturally say aloud

### Styling

- generic dark-tech black with saturated orange, blue or purple
- AI-purple gradients, glows, glass panels or decorative mesh
- automatic cream-paper scrapbook styling because FreTo used it
- arbitrary multicolour accents with no information role
- a default font, radius or shadow system left untouched because it looks polished
- decorative lines, dots, pills, arrows or UI chrome that carry no meaning

### Product and slide behaviour

- a dashboard, chatbot or mobile app selected before the medium is justified
- fake interface detail used as visual texture
- animation added without a named communication job
- a deck that documents process instead of changing the audience's confidence
- research artifacts shown as decoration rather than evidence that changed a decision

## Two decisive tests

### Logo-swap test

Replace the product name and copy mentally. If the visual direction still fits a finance app, AI startup, design agency and sustainability pitch equally well, it fails.

### Team-voice test

Read every visible line aloud. If the team would feel unnatural saying it, remove or rewrite it. A design cannot feel authored when its language does not.

## Interestingness requirement

Anti-AI does not mean neutral, empty or conventionally tasteful. The artifact must contain at least one content-native surprise: an unusual scale change, sequence, material, interaction, image treatment or information structure that makes the subject easier to feel or understand.

The surprise fails when it is only decoration, when it could be pasted onto another project, or when it reduces legibility.

## Post-build subtraction pass

Inspect the render at thumbnail size and at 100 percent. Then:

1. Remove every label, container and accent that does not change understanding.
2. Rewrite every line that sounds generated or slogan-like.
3. Check that the first visual impression matches the communication job.
4. Confirm the signature move remains clear after subtraction.
5. Name at least one material human change made after seeing the render.
6. Re-run the logo-swap and team-voice tests.

## Verdict

Return one result:

- **Pass:** the work has a subject-specific point of view, survives both decisive tests and has no unresolved material AI tell.
- **Revise:** only the named corrective edit may proceed. Use this when the idea is sound but the render is generic, over-designed or under-authored.
- **Reject:** stop the direction when it is subject-blind, reference imitation, fabricated evidence, or a generated first draft that nobody has meaningfully selected or changed.

State the strongest remaining AI tell, the content-native signature, the human-authored change and the exact next action. Do not assign an authenticity score.
