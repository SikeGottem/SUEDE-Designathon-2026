# Team catch-up

- **The brief**
  - Design an experience that **reveals, communicates, questions or redesigns an unwritten rule**.
  - The outcome does not have to be an app. It could be digital, physical, social, spatial or a combination.

- **The unwritten rule we are exploring**
  - Friends often care about and appreciate each other, but usually only express it directly when there is a recognised occasion.
  - Birthdays, farewells, crises and major milestones give people permission to say meaningful things.
  - On an ordinary day, the same message can feel unusually intense, romantic, alarming or like a goodbye.
  - A meaningful gesture can also make the receiver feel that they need to give an equally meaningful response.

- **The problem**
  - The appreciation already exists; the difficult part is expressing it in a way that feels natural, sincere and comfortable for both people.
  - For the sender:
    - It can be hard to know when or how to say it.
    - They may worry that the message will be misunderstood.
    - They may want it to feel more deliberate than an ordinary text without becoming a huge gesture.
  - For the receiver:
    - They may wonder why the message has arrived unexpectedly.
    - They may misread the sender's intention.
    - They may feel pressure to reply with the same emotional weight.
  - Existing communication options seem to sit at opposite ends:
    - **Ordinary messaging** is convenient, but meaningful words can feel casual or disappear inside everyday chat.
    - **Physical letters and handmade gifts** feel intentional and keepable, but require more time, access and delivery effort.
  - This will not affect every friendship. Some people already express care comfortably through words, humour, practical help, time or shared activities.

- **The direction of the experience**
  - Create a socially understood container for expressing appreciation on an ordinary day.
  - We are currently exploring a deliberately made **digital keepsake** that sits between a casual message and a physical letter.
  - It could let a sender combine personal writing with photographs, voice, music, video or drawing.
  - It should feel authored by the sender, not generated from a generic template.
  - It should give the receiver something personal they can keep without forcing them to reply or return the gesture.
  - Digital is only a working direction. We still need to prove that it adds something a normal message, physical letter or simpler ritual cannot.

- **What has been decided**
  - Friendship appreciation is our working problem space.
  - The important moment is expressing meaningful appreciation **without waiting for a special occasion**.
  - Both the sender's hesitation and the receiver's interpretation need to be designed for.
  - The experience should support genuine human expression rather than write intimate content for people.

- **What has not been decided**
  - The exact audience or friendship situation.
  - The final format, interaction or delivery method.
  - Whether the strongest answer is digital, physical or hybrid.
  - The product features, visual identity and final pitch story.

- **What we need to prove next**
  - That this problem occurs beyond our own team and personal experiences.
  - What actually creates the discomfort: lack of occasion, fear of misinterpretation, emotional vulnerability, reply pressure or something else.
  - Whether a designed digital object feels more meaningful than the same words sent through ordinary chat.
  - Whether receivers experience the gesture as warm and welcome rather than awkward, alarming or demanding.
