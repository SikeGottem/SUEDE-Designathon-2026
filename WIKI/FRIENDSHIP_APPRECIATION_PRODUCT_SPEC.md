## 1. How to read this draft

- **Current instruction** — Ethan has directly chosen this for the next prototype.

- **Proposed for review** — a coherent answer is needed to show the full experience; the team can change it.

## 2. The concept in one sentence

> A person turns a real thought about someone they value into a small, private digital care package; their friend opens it as an intentional object rather than a chat message, experiences the words and memories at their own pace, and decides what it becomes next.

The shortest retelling is:

> **Make something personal. Give it once. Let them receive it without owing you a performance.**

The second line is internal product logic, not final marketing copy.

## 3. What is being redesigned

### The working unwritten rule

On an ordinary day, direct appreciation between friends is not always expected. Without a birthday, farewell, crisis, or another accepted occasion, a sincere expression can feel unusually intense, romantic, farewell-like, alarming, or as though it asks for an equally meaningful response.

### The moment being changed

The appreciation already exists. The difficult sequence is:

```text
notice something meaningful
→ consider saying it
→ wonder whether it will feel weird or too intense
→ struggle to find a fitting channel
→ either flatten it into chat or leave it unsaid
```

The prototype changes the channel and receiving contract:

```text
notice something meaningful
→ make a finite personal object
→ give it privately
→ the friend opens it when ready
→ the object ends without demanding a reply
→ the friend decides whether it stays
```

### What this is not claiming

- People have stopped caring about their friends.
- Text, humour, presence, actions, gifts, voice notes, or physical letters are inferior.
- Everyone wants explicit sentimental language.
- Men, Australians, or young people all share one friendship rule.
- A polished digital object automatically reduces awkwardness or reply pressure.

## 4. Product promise

The experience should make four things true at once:

1. **The sender's effort is visible.** They choose the words, materials, arrangement, pacing, and personal marks.
2. **Digital earns its place.** The object can hold voice, images, music, motion, replay, distance, and accessible alternatives in ways ordinary chat does not combine.
3. **Receiving is a designed experience.** The friend crosses a clear threshold into a private object rather than landing in another feed or thread.
4. **The receiver owns the ending.** They may take time, keep it, close it, or remove it without the sender monitoring them.

If the interface only helps someone type a message into a prettier container, it has missed the concept.

## 5. Non-negotiable experience principles

### 5.1 Human-authored intimacy

The sender writes or records the personal content. AI may ask reflective questions, organise drafts privately, or help build the interface; it does not generate the final appreciation in the sender's voice.

### 5.2 One object, not a conversation

The artifact has a beginning, an opening, a body, and an ending. There are no message bubbles, reply composer, comment thread, typing indicator, reaction count, or read receipt in the experience.

### 5.3 Creative, not bureaucratic

The creator should feel like a full-screen camera and blank notebook combined: as immediate as making a Story, but still one finite authored object. Recipient, writing, captured media, and selected materials live together in one evolving composition instead of becoming a sequence of form pages.

### 5.4 Deliberate, not artificially difficult

Meaning comes from the sender's choices. Forced waiting, quotas, scarcity, streaks, and decorative busywork do not simulate sincerity.

### 5.5 Receiver-first after handoff

Once given, the object is no longer a sender dashboard item. The sender cannot watch whether it was opened, replayed, kept, closed, or removed.

### 5.6 Richness with a point

Each photograph, recording, song, animation, drawing, or mark must carry a memory, a voice, an authorship cue, or a state change. It cannot exist merely to make the product look feature-rich.

### 5.7 A real ending

Closing is not failure. Removing is not rejection. Keeping is not the morally correct response. None of these choices sends a relational signal back.

## 6. Who the first experience is for

### Proposed prototype pair

One known person making something for one intact close or still-close friend on an ordinary day, after a mundane but meaningful thought or shared moment.

This keeps the first story away from apology, reconciliation, romance, grief, crisis, or unwanted contact, where the same interaction has different safety and interpretation needs.

### Sender need

> “I already know what I value about this person. Help me turn it into something that feels considered and unmistakably mine.”

### Receiver need

> “Let me understand who this is from, open it privately when I choose, experience it without performing a response, and decide whether it remains with me.”

### Open audience question

The long-term audience is not yet selected. Close friends, low-contact but intact friends, long-distance friends, and family were all discussed. “Young men” remains a research lens, not the product identity.

## 7. What the digital object contains

### Required centre

- one known maker;
- one intended person;
- one specific reason, memory, quality, or moment;
- the maker's own written or spoken core;
- one coherent composition;
- one private ending controlled by the receiver.

### Expressive material palette

The product may let the maker use:

- **words** — the main note, small captions, labels, or a sign-off;
- **photographs** — a shared place, person, object, screenshot, or memory;
- **voice** — a recording that preserves cadence and can be replayed later;
- **music** — a song connected to the person or moment;
- **short video** — a moving memory where still imagery is not enough;
- **drawing** — a doodle, diagram, line, or handmade symbol;
- **marks** — stickers, stamp, signature, seal, underline, arrow, or divider;
- **arrangement** — scale, spacing, order, overlap, and sequence as part of the expression.

### Proposed first example

To express the actual vision, the first realistic sample object should contain:

1. one written core;
2. one photograph;
3. one short voice recording;
4. one song reference or playable music fragment;
5. one sender-made mark or drawing.

This is an experience demonstration, not a claim that every object needs five materials. The product must still allow a simpler object to feel complete.

### Optional reflective cues

The creator can ask for prompts such as:

- a favourite memory;
- something this person taught me;
- a quality I notice;
- one word I associate with them;
- a small moment I did not say enough about.

These cues appear only when requested. They are sparks, not mandatory fields, templates, or AI-written answers.

## 8. The overall emotional arc

```text
WHITE — an open place to make
    ↓
WHITE — a finished object waiting to be given
    ↓
WHITE — a known sender and a sealed arrival
    ↓ deliberate opening
NAVY — a private world that belongs to the receiver
    ↓
NAVY — a memory that can end or be revisited
```

White is not just a light theme. It represents possibility, work, and the unopened state.

Navy is not just a dark theme. It represents attention, privacy, depth, and the object becoming the receiver's experience.

The change happens at opening. It is the product's main visual state transition.

## 9. Page map

The conceptual product has seven page families. Modes inside the creator remain on one page so the experience does not become a wizard.

```text
1. Studio
   └─ capture or blank paper → compose → arrange
2. Preview and seal
3. Handoff

4. Arrival
5. Opening threshold
6. Full object
7. Private cabinet (optional branch)
```

## 10. Page 1 — Studio

**Status:** Current exploratory direction, corrected by the newest 4 September interaction review and Ethan's direct 5 September clarification; details remain proposed for human testing.

### Purpose

Turn a genuine thought into a composed object without making the person feel like they are filling out a greeting-card form.

### First impression

Creation takes over the whole phone. It feels as immediate as making an Instagram Story, while keeping this product's own white/navy, handwritten, private-object language. The paper is the full-screen creation field rather than a bordered card inside another page, and ordinary app chrome recedes.

The initial surface may be a live camera or blank white paper. Photo and video capture are immediately available; the maker can also choose an existing moment or begin without media. There is no feed, dashboard, archive, friend grid, template market, or product tour competing for attention.

### How it begins

- Entering the Studio exposes capture immediately rather than asking the maker to complete a form first.
- One tap takes a photograph; switching to video makes the same central control start and stop filming.
- Permission failure never traps the maker: retry, choose a device photo/video, use the labelled sample, and blank paper reach the same next state.
- Successful capture moves directly into composition without an upload-confirmation or settings screen.
- The recipient remains a light-touch editable anchor at the top and does not become a separate full-screen step or progress bar.

### How making works conceptually

- Captured photo/video becomes the page itself; blank paper stays a first-class alternative.
- A compact, hand-drawn tool rail offers words, camera, voice, song and drawing at thumb reach without becoming a Canva toolbar.
- Selecting a material lands it directly on the canvas with visible state feedback.
- Text and material pieces are selected in place, dragged directly, rotated from one small contextual handle, edited or removed. Controls disappear when the piece is not selected.
- Text editing temporarily takes focus, with an optional reflective cue such as “favourite memory,” “what they taught you,” “one word,” or “one small thing you notice.” The cue never writes the appreciation for the maker.
- A brief first-use gesture cue may explain move/rotate, then disappears; no permanent hint competes with the work.
- A piece may overlap another when the composition still reads clearly, but the first canvas is not pre-populated with competing fragments.
- Camera re-entry, text editing, placement and rotation preserve the same draft when the maker returns from Preview.
- Autosaving or backend recovery remain outside the conceptual UI unless later testing shows they are necessary.

### What should be felt

Freedom with enough constraint to stay intimate. The maker should see their own taste and relationship in the object, not feel they selected a template generated for everyone.

### Transition

When the composition feels finished, one `done` action moves to the preview. There is no percentage complete, upload wizard, or system judgment of whether the message is emotional enough.

### Must never appear

- separate recipient, reason, writing, media, and colour form pages;
- AI “write it for me” or tone buttons;
- occasion categories as the main starting point;
- an infinite Canva-style feature wall;
- copied Instagram branding, feed/story expiry, views, reactions, replies, or social posting;
- visible full-time media controls, prompt chips, drag hints, and sample fragments competing with the writing area;
- equal rounded cards for every material;
- public templates ranked by popularity;
- friend suggestions, daily assignments, reminders, or streaks;
- relationship scores or “effort” meters.

## 11. Page 2 — Preview and seal

**Status:** Proposed for review.

### Purpose

Let the maker stop editing and experience the composition as one finished thing before giving it away.

### What appears

- the complete object without editing controls;
- the recipient and maker identity;
- the authored order in which pieces will be encountered;
- any sound or motion only after the maker actively previews it;
- one path back to editing;
- one action to finish or seal.

### The object boundary

Finishing gives the composition a clear edge. For the first prototype, the recommended expression is one folded or sealed hand-drawn object rather than a menu of bottles, birds, boxes, and themes.

The sender may add one personal mark: a signature, stamp, seal symbol, or small drawing. It should signal authorship, not status or rarity.

### Preview colour

The surrounding maker page remains white. “See it as they will” may briefly enter the navy receiver view, then return to white when editing resumes. Navy is not used as a decorative sender theme.

### What should be felt

The shift from working draft to something ready to give.

### Must never appear

- “your friend will love this” or another predicted reaction;
- engagement forecasts or emotional scoring;
- fake delivered, seen, or opened states;
- confetti for finishing;
- pressure to add more material;
- a package marketplace.

## 12. Page 3 — Handoff

**Status:** Strong direction.

### Purpose

Let the maker give the object privately, then finish their side of the experience.

### What happens

- The product creates or simulates one private link.
- The maker chooses how to pass that link outside the product.
- A concise departure gesture may show the sealed object leaving.
- The maker sees a calm completion state with no receiver activity.

The practical interaction may be “copy link” or a device share action, but the emotional framing is “give this,” not “publish” or “send a message.”

### Sender ending

After handoff, the maker can leave or make something else. There is no inbox row that later changes to opened, kept, ignored, or replied.

### Must never appear

- read receipts;
- opened or replayed counts;
- follow-up reminders;
- “nudge them”;
- a countdown that makes waiting equal sincerity;
- public posting;
- a reply expected state.

## 13. Page 4 — Arrival

**Status:** Strong direction; exact object metaphor open.

### Purpose

Give the receiver enough trust and control to decide whether this is a welcome moment.

### First impression

The page is still white. One sealed, hand-drawn object waits in generous space. The receiver sees who made it before intimate content is revealed.

Suggested information hierarchy:

1. sender identity;
2. a plain statement that they made something private;
3. the sealed object;
4. open when ready;
5. a quiet not-now/leave route.

### What should be felt

Curiosity without alarm, urgency, or public performance. It should evoke the moment of noticing a personal letter or parcel with their name on it.

### Optional arrival cue

A firefly may appear as one small light that draws attention to the object. It only survives if it explains arrival or where to act. It must not become a mascot, pet, streak mechanic, or decorative swarm.

### Must never appear

- anonymous delivery;
- intimate content preview;
- urgent animation or countdown;
- autoplay audio;
- an unread badge that grows;
- a forced scan, sign-in, or app install;
- copy implying that a good friend opens immediately.

## 14. Page 5 — Opening threshold

**Status:** Current visual instruction; motion details proposed for review.

### Purpose

Separate notification from receiving and make the white-to-navy transition meaningful.

### Sequence

1. The receiver chooses to open.
2. The seal or object responds under their touch.
3. The white page recedes, unfolds, or is covered.
4. Deep navy becomes the full field.
5. Off-white handwriting and linework arrive with the object.
6. The first authored words become readable without waiting for spectacle.

The animation should feel hand-made and tactile: a line being drawn, paper unfolding, a mark breaking, or the object gently expanding. It is brief and skippable.

### What should be felt

“I have entered the thing they made for me.”

### Must never appear

- generic app page transitions;
- AI-style particles, glows, glass, or cinematic gradients;
- a long unskippable intro;
- sound before consent;
- motion without an equivalent direct opening path.

## 15. Page 6 — Full object

**Status:** Strong direction; exact composition grammar proposed for review.

### Purpose

Let the receiver absorb one personal object away from chat and product chrome.

### First impression

Deep navy fills the screen. Warm off-white handwriting and hand-drawn marks create the composition. Navigation disappears or becomes nearly invisible. The object, not the app, is the whole experience.

### Recommended composition model: the living page

The object is one continuous authored surface rather than a slide deck, chat thread, stack of equal cards, or dashboard. The receiver moves through the composition in the order the maker set, with occasional tap-to-reveal or tap-to-play moments.

- The written core appears first or anchors the whole surface.
- Photographs feel placed, taped, framed, or held—not dropped into generic cards.
- Voice appears as a small hand-drawn playable object with a transcript available.
- Music waits for an explicit play action and remains subordinate to the message.
- Video uses a clear still and never auto-plays.
- Doodles, arrows, captions, and marks connect pieces where the maker chose them.
- Empty navy space is allowed; the product does not fill every gap.

### Pacing

The receiver controls progress. Motion may reveal relationships between pieces, but it never forces a fixed emotional tempo. Words stay readable and media stays replayable.

### What should be felt

Presence, specificity, and the accumulated evidence that someone noticed and made choices for them.

### Must never appear

- chat bubbles, comments, reactions, or reply composer;
- product navigation competing with the object;
- generic feature cards;
- auto-playing sound or video;
- system-written sentimental copy;
- prompts to share the object publicly;
- sender-visible receiver actions;
- a success score or “friendship health” state.

## 16. Page 7 — Receiver ending and private cabinet

**Status:** Strong desire with an unresolved persistence model.

### Purpose

Make ownership and ending real rather than claiming “no pressure” in copy.

### Ending choices

- **Keep** — the object enters the receiver's private cabinet.
- **Close** — the experience ends quietly without making a permanent choice.
- **Remove** — the object leaves their view after a plain confirmation.

None of these actions informs the maker. None is styled as more caring.

### The private cabinet

If the receiver keeps the object, they return to a deep-navy memory surface rather than an inbox. Objects can be reopened as distinct personal things. A voice recording from a year ago should still feel like the same object, not a buried chat attachment.

The cabinet should feel closer to a memory box, record shelf, or camera roll of meaningful artifacts than a social product home screen.

### What appears

- a small number of visually distinct objects;
- maker identity and an optional human date label;
- the personal mark or silhouette of each object;
- quiet replay and removal paths.

### Must never appear

- unread counts;
- sorting by best friend, frequency, value, or effort;
- streaks or collection completion;
- public profiles;
- prompts to solicit more objects;
- sender analytics;
- comparison between relationships.

### Open questions

- Is keeping opt-in, automatic, temporary, or unnecessary?
- Can the maker revoke or edit after giving?
- Can the receiver export or forward?
- What happens after a wrong-recipient link or unwanted contact?

These must remain visible before a real service is claimed.

## 17. Visual language

### Current palette sequence

- **Creation and unopened state:** white field, very dark ink, occasional off-white paper distinction only when necessary.
- **Opened object and cabinet:** deep navy field, warm off-white writing and linework.
- **Accent use:** one small accent only when it explains focus, media, or state. Do not revive the earlier multi-colour book-cover palette yet.

### Typography

Use locally bundled Gaegu Regular as the current open-licensed Ugly Dave-like stand-in for headings, labels, captions, and the authored object in this prototype. Keep the prior local Ugly Handwriting font as a fallback, and keep the stack reversible while the team refines the final lettering. If a critical legal, safety, or accessibility line becomes hard to read, a restrained readable companion face may support it without replacing the main voice.

### Hand-drawn system

- Real team-drawn assets replace generic icon libraries.
- Lines can wobble, taper, miss perfect symmetry, and carry pressure variation.
- Imperfection comes from the hand, not random CSS rotation or fake noise.
- Every recurring icon is drawn in the same visual language.
- The recurring-object line weight must sit close to Gaegu's visible weight and confidence; neither hairline doodles nor heavy unrelated strokes pass as a coherent system.
- Team-drawn priority assets replace AI/code-generated stand-ins before the treatment is called final.
- Photographs can remain photographic; the product does not apply fake sketch filters to everything.

### Spatial rules

- Large areas of open space.
- One meaningful focal point at a time.
- Asymmetry and overlap where they show authorship.
- Strong silhouettes rather than bordered containers.
- No rounded-card grid, coloured side rail, stacked all-caps metadata, pills, glass panels, generic gradient, or bento layout.
- Controls disappear when the object should have attention.

### Reference delta

Borrow the supplied `grug` reference's handwriting, sparse placement, line quality, and confidence in one flat colour field. Do not borrow the `grug` name, flower, sunrise, navigation arrangement, copy, mascot, or exact composition.

## 18. Motion and sound language

Motion has four allowed jobs:

1. show that a piece was placed or arranged by the maker;
2. show that the object has left the maker;
3. show the receiver crossing from sealed white into private navy;
4. reveal a relationship between memory pieces or confirm a receiver-owned ending.

Potential motions:

- a rough line draws itself once;
- paper or an object gently unfolds;
- a seal separates;
- a photograph settles into position;
- a voice line pulses only while playing;
- a firefly lights once to indicate arrival;
- the cabinet accepts an object without celebration.

Avoid looping ambience, confetti, parallax, floating particles, springy SaaS micro-interactions, or animation added only to look expensive.

Sound begins only after a clear receiver action. Silence is the default.

### Presentation constraint

The pitch must make sense in silence. Do not depend on a song, ambient audio, or an audio-only explanation to make the product or the demo understandable. For presentation construction, set the narrative and slide layout before using AI to refine individual visual treatments; AI is a bounded production aid, not the author of the deck's structure.

## 19. Product voice

The product should sound plain, warm, and unperformed.

- Short lines.
- No therapeutic diagnosis.
- No startup slogans.
- No fake poetry generated by the system.
- No claims that the sender is brave or the receiver is lucky.
- No guilt around opening, keeping, or replying.
- No excessive explanation of the product's emotional intention.

The sender's words supply personality. System copy should mostly create permission and get out of the way.

## 20. What does not exist in this product

- a social feed;
- a public profile;
- follower/friend graph;
- anonymous messages;
- random recipient selection;
- daily gratitude prompts;
- streaks, scores, badges, or levels;
- relationship health tracking;
- sender-visible opens, keeps, replays, or removals;
- reply, comments, or reactions inside the object;
- AI-generated appreciation;
- a marketplace of packages or themes in the first prototype;
- forced delivery delay;
- an app-install requirement for the receiver;
- male-only positioning;
- mental-health outcome claims;
- fabricated research or personalised judge surveillance.

## 21. Structural directions considered before styling

### A. The living page — recommended

The maker composes one continuous surface. The receiver enters a full-screen navy version of that same authored world.

- **First notice:** the person's words and spatial composition.
- **Makes clear:** this is one object, not a sequence of app screens.
- **Risk:** it can become a generic scrapbook editor if controls dominate.
- **Source anchor:** layout and creative decisions were described as part of the communication itself.

### B. The pocket of fragments

The maker places small memory pieces into one container; the receiver draws them out one by one.

- **First notice:** the ritual of discovering separate pieces.
- **Makes clear:** the object contains more than a letter.
- **Risk:** gimmicky packaging can overpower the relationship.
- **Source anchor:** care-package, letter, envelope, bottle, and object metaphors.

### C. The threaded constellation

Words, images, voice, and song sit in open space connected by one hand-drawn line that guides the receiver.

- **First notice:** the relationship among memories rather than a literal package.
- **Makes clear:** digital can join different media into one path.
- **Risk:** it can become decorative or hard to navigate.
- **Source anchor:** sparse hand-drawn style, motion, and a camera-roll-like memory surface.

### Current recommendation

Use **A. The living page** as the conceptual base. Do not blend in B or C until Ethan and the team choose a specific element and name the job it performs.

## 22. Design-authenticity preflight

- **Communication job:** make authored effort, the opening threshold, and receiver ownership felt without explaining them in labels.
- **Specific source anchors:** a real physical care package; the scrapbook/letter merge; replaying a familiar voice later; the receiver's private cabinet; the awkwardness of a heartfelt message inside chat.
- **Reference delta:** handmade confidence and spaciousness from `grug`, but a different identity, object model, colour sequence, content, and composition.
- **Rejected defaults:** form wizard, equal media cards, cream scrapbook collage, generic courier mascot, AI-dark gradient, decorative particles, feature dashboard.
- **Signature move:** the act of opening turns the whole world from white into deep navy; the object becomes the receiver's space.
- **Restraint:** no package chooser or mascot system in the first version.
- **Human decision:** Ethan rejected the narrow generated prototype, required a transcript-first restart, and directly selected white then navy, Ugly Handwriting, and the supplied handmade reference grammar.
- **Preflight verdict:** **Pass for conceptual development.** The actual render still requires a post-build authenticity review and a material human change before it can become the visual source of truth.

## 23. Pitch and demo relationship

The working prototype can become the proof moment in the pitch:

1. Explain the ordinary-day appreciation tension simply.
2. Show how the maker turns a thought into an object.
3. Show the receiver crossing from white arrival into the navy object.
4. End with a generic QR/private link that lets willing audience members open one shared example.

The room interaction demonstrates the flow only. A scan, smile, reply, or emotional reaction is not evidence that the product changes a norm.

The QR route must be optional and have a no-device alternative. It must not use private judge research or demand personal content.

## 24. Main conceptual risks

### “It is just a digital card or scrapbook”

The answer cannot be “ours looks better.” The difference must be visible in authored composition, the opening threshold, the absence of a reply loop, and receiver-owned ending.

### Rich media becomes feature theatre

The experience fails if photo, voice, music, video, and animation appear only to impress judges. Each piece must reveal something specific about the person or memory.

### No reply makes the message more ominous

Removing chat infrastructure may reduce pressure, or it may heighten alarm. This remains the central receiver test.

### The cabinet creates emotional or storage burden

Keeping must stay optional. A receiver may want the object to disappear, expire, export, or remain outside a collection.

### Handmade becomes an aesthetic costume

Ugly Handwriting and doodles cannot manufacture authenticity. The content and arrangement must actually come from the maker, and team-drawn assets must have product jobs.

### The product normalises one preferred friendship language

Some people communicate care through humour, action, presence, or silence. The product serves people who want this form; it should not grade other friendships.

## 25. What the first prototype should help the team decide

The prototype is useful if it lets the team answer:

1. Does one living creator surface feel expressive or overwhelming?
2. Does the finished result feel like one object rather than a decorated web page?
3. Does the white-to-navy opening create the intended receiving threshold?
4. Which material adds real memory value beyond the written core?
5. Does the receiver feel freer without a reply path, or more uncertain?
6. Do keep, close, and remove feel genuinely private and equally acceptable?
7. Does a cabinet make the object more meaningful or create pressure?
8. Can someone explain the product without calling it a digital card?

It does not prove demand, prevalence, emotional benefit, or long-term use.

## 27. Scoped implementation delta — carrier and receiving test

**Status:** Current for one reversible prototype test only. This is Ethan's newer direct implementation decision, informed by the 4 September team-design capture and supplied mockups; it does not claim live Figma was read and does not validate a future product direction.

The former approval of one folded/sealed object with no package picker is **superseded only for this test**. It remains the approved baseline outside the test and must not be recorded as rejected or as a validated product conclusion.

- **Carrier browse:** offer a small, hand-drawn set of carrier icons in a horizontal browse, so one selected object can be seen at a useful scale. Do not turn it into a marketplace, theme system, or dense chooser.
- **Carrier field:** show the selected carrier by itself in generous negative space. Remove ornamental carrier backdrop paths (including bottle waves and ladybug routes) and the bottom mascot/helper explanation; the object and a purposeful selection transition are sufficient.
- **Choreography:** the selected carrier changes the handoff, arrival, and opening choreography, rather than changing intimate content or adding a different product flow.
- **Bottle path:** test a bottle with a direct cork swipe/drag opening. If that gesture is unavailable, unclear, or reduced motion is requested, provide a simple explicit open control that reaches the same content immediately.
- **Ladybug path:** a ladybug may make one dotted, one-time courier/notification journey. It is not a persistent mascot, companion, navigation device, collection mechanic, or brand identity.
- **Navigation and cabinet:** no bottom navigation. The optional private cabinet presents kept items as distinct physical objects, not generic rows, cards, or a feed.
