## 1. How to read this specification

Every statement uses one of five evidence labels:

- **Locked:** a current team decision or hard event/safety constraint.
- **Prototype condition:** a deliberate choice for the next test, not a claim that the final product should work this way.
- **Hypothesis:** plausible and worth testing, but not established as user truth.
- **Open:** materially unresolved; the prototype must keep it reversible.
- **Rejected:** excluded because it conflicts with the brief, evidence, safety, authenticity, or the chosen test.

The transcripts are source evidence, not automatic decisions. Repetition in overlapping recordings is not independent validation. A feature mentioned enthusiastically remains a proposal until the team selects it.

## 2. One-page definition

### 2.1 The unwritten rule

**Locked as the working rule:**

> On an ordinary day, friends are not expected to make a direct, deliberate expression of appreciation. Without a birthday, farewell, crisis, or other accepted occasion, the same message can feel unusually intense, romantic, farewell-like, alarming, or as though it asks for an equally meaningful response.

This is the rule being investigated. It is not yet validated as a broad Australian, generational, or gendered norm.

### 2.2 The problem

**Locked as the current problem frame:** appreciation often already exists. The difficult moment is when a person notices something they value about a friend, considers expressing it, chooses a channel, and predicts how the act will be interpreted.

The project must not claim that people have stopped caring about their friends. It must not treat action, humour, presence, shared activity, or quiet reliability as inferior forms of care.

### 2.3 The candidate intervention

**Prototype condition:** a sender uses mobile web to make one finite, private, self-authored digital keepsake for one known friend. The receiver encounters a calm arrival, opens it privately, experiences the object outside a chat thread, and controls whether to keep, close, or discard it. The sender receives no relational telemetry.

The first prototype asks:

> Does a bounded receiving contract plus an object-like digital form make ordinary-day appreciation feel clearer and less conversational than the same content in chat?

### 2.4 What is actually distinctive

The distinctive mechanism is not “a prettier digital card.” It is:

1. the sender authors a specific act rather than answering a platform assignment;
2. the act arrives as a finite object rather than a conversational turn;
3. the receiver owns the ending;
4. the sender cannot monitor, score, or chase the response;
5. digital media is used only where it adds a form of memory that paper or chat does not provide as easily.

### 2.5 Current verdict

**Proceed with conditions:** build and test this only as a reversible stimulus. The problem territory has team support and a narrow research basis, but the audience, causal mechanism, preferred medium, persistence model, and effect on reply pressure remain unproven.

## 4. Strategic frame

### 4.1 Situation

Friends already use text, voice, memes, calls, gifts, practical help, physical letters, and shared activities to show care. Chat is accessible and flexible. Physical gestures can be singular and keepable. Neither is inherently shallow or authentic.

### 4.2 Complication

Some sincere expressions do not fit comfortably into ordinary chat. A sender may predict awkwardness or misreading. A receiver may interpret unusual intensity as a crisis, confession, farewell, or obligation. Occasion-based rituals supply a familiar social script; an ordinary day does not.

Published work supports a narrow sender prediction gap around gratitude and supportive messages. It does not establish local prevalence or prove that this digital form solves it.

### 4.3 Candidate resolution

Test a new receiving contract: one private object, clearly from a known person, with no conversation UI, no sender-visible receipt, and a receiver-controlled ending. Pair that contract with deliberate authorship and one optional memory material.

### 4.4 Causal chain to test

1. A specific appreciative thought occurs.
2. The sender anticipates social ambiguity or an unwanted conversation.
3. Existing channels feel mismatched: chat is turn-based; physical delivery is logistically heavy.
4. A finite digital object gives the act a legible beginning and ending.
5. Removing response infrastructure may lower the sense that a reply is part of the transaction.
6. Receiver control may let the appreciation land without forcing performance.

Steps 4–6 are hypotheses, not findings.

### 4.5 Falsification conditions

Stop or substantially reframe the direction if any of the following happens in matched testing:

- senders prefer ordinary text or a voice note and the object does not change their predicted awkwardness;
- receivers feel more pressure, alarm, romantic ambiguity, or performance demand in the object condition;
- people do not want the content to persist or be collected;
- the visual ritual is remembered, but the intended social contract is not understood;
- the same benefit comes from a simple chat label without the object experience;
- the strongest users actually need a repair, reminder, or maintenance tool rather than an appreciation object;
- digital adds spectacle but no necessary capability over physical or existing behaviours.

## 5. Actors and scope boundary

### 5.1 Sender

**Prototype participant:** a person who already has one specific thought, memory, quality, act, photograph, voice fragment, or song connected to a known friend.

The system does not assign the recipient, manufacture the feeling, diagnose the relationship, or decide that the sender “should” reach out.

### 5.2 Receiver

**Co-equal user:** a known friend who receives an unexpected private object. They control attention, opening, persistence, disposal, and any future conversation outside the experience.

The receiver is not a passive reveal target and is not responsible for validating the sender’s effort.

### 5.3 Provisional test relationship

**Prototype condition:** two close or still-close friends whose relationship is intact and where contact is welcome. The message is sent on an ordinary day after a mundane but meaningful moment.

This is a neutral test scenario, not an ICP claim.

### 5.4 Out of scope for the first test

- unknown or anonymous recipients;
- former partners, estranged friends, conflict repair, apology, or reconciliation;
- crisis intervention, grief support, or mental-health treatment;
- workplace performance recognition;
- public or group appreciation;
- romantic declarations;
- children as primary users;
- contact where the receiver has asked for distance;
- one-sided pursuit or boundary circumvention.

### 5.5 Audience hypotheses to investigate later

- close friends after relocation or life transition;
- low-contact but intact friendships;
- friends whose normal register uses humour rather than explicit sentiment;
- long-distance friends or family;
- people who already save messages, photographs, voice notes, or letters.

“Young men” is a research context, not the default target. Recruit across genders, cultures, communication styles, and friendship rhythms.

## 6. Jobs to be done

### 6.1 Sender job

> When I notice a real and specific reason I value this friend on an ordinary day, help me turn my own words and one memory material into a bounded act that feels intentional without forcing a live emotional conversation.

### 6.2 Receiver job

> When unexpected appreciation arrives, let me understand who it is from, open it privately when I choose, experience it without a response task, and decide whether it remains with me.

### 6.3 Research job

> Hold the message as constant as possible and test whether the receiving contract—not visual polish alone—changes warmth, alarm, perceived obligation, authenticity, and desire to revisit.

## 7. Non-negotiable design principles

### P-01 — Authored, not automated

The sender writes the intimate content. AI may organise research, ask reflective questions, check clarity, or build disposable interfaces; it must not generate the final personal appreciation on the sender’s behalf.

### P-02 — Finite, not conversational

The object has a clear beginning and ending. No message bubbles, reply box, typing indicator, read receipt, chat thread, or “continue the conversation” call to action appears in the critical path.

### P-03 — Receiver agency is structural

Keep, close, defer, and discard must be real choices. None sends a relational signal back. Copy alone cannot claim “no pressure” while the system still exposes response state.

### P-04 — No relational telemetry

The sender does not learn whether the receiver opened, kept, replayed, closed, or discarded the object. There are no engagement scores, reminders to follow up, relationship-health indicators, or effort comparisons.

### P-05 — Digital must earn its place

The prototype uses digital for accountless distance, controlled reveal, accessible alternatives, and one replayable memory material. It must not claim superiority over text, voice, physical mail, gifts, or in-person care before comparison.

### P-06 — Deliberate effort, not artificial friction

Specificity and authorship should require thought. Forced waiting, arbitrary scarcity, quotas, streaks, or decorative steps must not be used to simulate sincerity.

### P-07 — Known sender, private arrival

The receiver can identify the sender before opening. No anonymous surprise, public wall, social graph, discovery feed, or personalised judge research.

### P-08 — Richness stays subordinate

Writing is the required core. One optional supporting material can deepen the memory. More media is not assumed to mean more meaning.

### P-09 — Craft must explain the interaction

Handwriting, the seal, courier, shelf, colour, motion, and sound each need a narrative job. Anything that does not clarify authorship, arrival, opening, ownership, or ending is removed.

### P-10 — Evidence remains honest

No fabricated survey, quote, persona, statistic, test, emotional reaction, or validation may enter the prototype, wiki, appendix, Devpost page, deck, or pitch. AI-generated synthesis is labelled as synthesis; human claims require traceable human evidence.

## 8. Selected prototype proposition

### 8.1 Form factor

**Prototype condition:** mobile-first web experience opened from a private link. This avoids App Store friction and allows a real receiver flow without requiring an account.

It is not a final architecture decision. The prototype must remain asset-pluggable and easy to restyle after Cecelia’s brand system is locked.

### 8.2 Product metaphor

**Prototype condition:** a finite keepsake with a restrained postal-object vocabulary.

- “Letter” is an understandable content affordance, not the entire product category.
- A seal makes the unopened/opened state legible.
- A stamp or small mark can carry sender-specific authorship.
- A hand-drawn firefly may signal arrival because it lights or carries attention; it is optional and must not become a pet, streak, or notification game.
- The shelf makes receiver ownership visible, but persistence remains a hypothesis.

Bottle, bird, firefly, envelope, scrapbook, poster, cabinet, stork, carrier pigeon, travel/migration mascots, and selectable packages were all discussed. They must not all ship as themes in the first test. One coherent expression is used; the rest remain reference branches. The team explicitly rejected choosing an obvious mascot merely because it maps literally to delivery.

### 8.3 Content minimum

The object contains:

1. one known sender;
2. one intended receiver;
3. one specific reason or context;
4. one self-authored written core;
5. zero or one supporting material;
6. one receiver-controlled ending.

Text-only remains complete.

### 8.4 Supporting material in the first build

The interface may demonstrate photo and voice affordances, but one object contains no more than one selected supporting material. Every non-text material needs an accessible text equivalent.

Music, video, drawing, custom handwriting, animation, and richer composition remain later branches until the core receiving contract works.

## 9. Functional scope

### 9.1 P0 — must work in the next prototype

- Start a new keepsake without a daily prompt or assigned friend.
- Enter one known friend and one specific reason.
- Write the main content in the sender’s own words.
- Add or skip one supporting material.
- Preview one finite object, not a chat message.
- Simulate a private share link without claiming real delivery.
- End the sender journey without a seen/reply state.
- Show a receiver arrival that names the known sender before opening.
- Allow the receiver to defer or leave.
- Open through one purposeful reveal; reduced motion can skip it.
- Read or play the material with an equivalent transcript/description.
- Keep, close, or discard without signalling the sender.
- Confirm destructive discard and allow recovery in the prototype session.
- Preserve a sender draft if sharing fails or the user navigates back.
- Clearly label simulated transfer, storage, and identity.

### 9.2 P1 — useful test branches, not required in the critical path

- A receiver shelf containing one or two sample keepsakes.
- Structural closure alone versus structural closure plus explicit “nothing is expected back” copy.
- A text-only object versus the same text with one voice or photo material.
- A static seal versus an animated opening.
- A neutral mark versus a firefly arrival cue.
- Optional local-only custom stamp or wax-seal symbol.
- A neutral shell with one fixed object colour versus a sender-selected swatch from the restrained palette.
- Private acknowledgement as an alternate experiment, disabled by default and never sender-visible in the main condition.

### 9.3 Deferred

- real authentication or accounts;
- cloud persistence;
- native mobile applications;
- contact import or address book integration;
- real notifications;
- real uploads, streaming, or media transcoding;
- multiple people contributing to one object;
- printed fulfilment or physical hardware;
- a broad creator canvas or full scrapbook editor;
- multiple packages, delivery animals, or a theme marketplace;
- unrestricted custom colour selection;
- monetisation;
- relationship maintenance features.

### 9.4 Rejected

- AI-written intimate copy;
- daily assignments, random friend selection, or gratitude streaks;
- “people you should message” reminders;
- engagement, effort, or relationship scores;
- read receipts, reaction counts, sender-visible opens, or replay counts;
- reply composer or chat thread after opening;
- public feeds, searchable profiles, discovery, or social ranking;
- anonymous delivery;
- artificial delay presented as inherently meaningful;
- forced scarcity or message quotas;
- male-only positioning;
- mental-health outcome claims;
- compulsory QR participation;
- personalising the pitch from private judge research;
- fabricated primary research or fake survey results.

## 10. End-to-end user journey

### 10.1 Sender journey

| Stage | User intent | Required experience | Must not happen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Trigger | Act on a thought they already had | A quiet, self-initiated “make one” entry | Random recipient, guilt, reminder, streak |
| Person | Name who the object is for | Known recipient field; local prototype data only | Contact scraping or discovery |
| Reason | Anchor the act in something specific | One short reason, memory, quality, or moment | AI-generated sentiment or generic occasion template |
| Compose | Put the appreciation in their own words | Large writing space, clear draft preservation | Chat bubble or predictive emotional script |
| Material | Add one piece of memory evidence | Skip, photo, or voice; one selected maximum | “More is better” checklist or media overload |
| Review | See the act as a complete object | Full object preview with sender/receiver context | Recipient reaction simulation |
| Transfer | Pass it privately | Simulated share link and honest status | False “delivered,” “seen,” or account claims |
| Exit | Finish giving | Sender-terminal confirmation with no tracking | Nudge to wait for or chase a response |

### 10.2 Receiver journey

| Stage | User intent | Required experience | Must not happen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Arrival | Decide whether the link is safe and welcome | Known sender, plain object description, calm tone | Hidden identity, urgency, content preview leakage |
| Defer | Choose another moment or decline attention | Close/leave without consequence | Countdown, unread badge, reminder, sender signal |
| Open | Begin intentionally | Seal/reveal interaction or direct reduced-motion path | Auto-play audio or spectacle before consent |
| Experience | Absorb the words and one material | Object fills attention; controls are sparse | Chat chrome, reactions, comments, reply box |
| End | Decide what this becomes | Keep, close, or discard | Forced acknowledgement or guilt copy |
| Revisit | Return only if wanted | Private shelf in the persistence test branch | Sender-visible history or relationship score |

## 11. State model and invariants

### 11.1 Sender states

```text
idle
  → recipient_selected
  → reason_added
  → body_authored
  → material_skipped | material_added
  → previewed
  → share_attempted
  → share_failed | sender_complete
```

### 11.2 Receiver states

```text
arrival_unopened
  → deferred
  → opened
  → experiencing
  → kept | closed | discard_pending
  → discarded | discard_cancelled
```

### 11.3 Invariants

- `sender_complete` never transitions to seen, opened, kept, replied, or ignored.
- Receiver state is never displayed to the sender.
- `closed` is not equal to `kept`.
- `discarded` is receiver-private.
- A text-only object can reach preview and completion.
- No media auto-plays before the receiver chooses to open and play it.
- Reduced-motion mode reaches the same content and ending without loss.
- A failed share preserves the complete local draft.
- A receiver doing nothing is a valid outcome.

## 12. Screen specification

### S-01 — Quiet shelf / entry

**Purpose:** establish that these are finite, receiver-owned objects and provide one clear starting action.

**Content:** sparse shelf, at most two realistic sample keepsakes, “make one” action, explicit exploratory label outside the artifact.

**Rules:** no feed, unread count, streak, friend grid, engagement metric, or guilt-based empty state. The shelf may be removed if tests show persistence is unwanted.

### S-02 — Person and reason

**Purpose:** establish specificity before surface design.

**Required fields:** recipient display name and one plain-language reason/moment. The prototype may use local sample names but must not imply contact access.

**Progression:** continue remains disabled until both fields contain intentional input. Validation explains what is missing without judging the user’s emotion.

### S-03 — Write

**Purpose:** make the sender’s words the centre of the act.

**Layout:** one generous writing surface. No multiple tiny cards, dashboard controls, tone selector, AI button, template carousel, or “make it heartfelt” generator.

**Draft behaviour:** preserve locally during back navigation and simulated transfer failure.

### S-04 — Add one material

**Purpose:** demonstrate digital-specific memory without turning the experience into an editor.

**Choices:** skip, add one photo, or add one voice recording. If both are shown, selecting one deselects the other.

**Accessibility:** photo requires a description; voice requires transcript text. Record/play controls must visibly change state and remain keyboard-operable.

### S-05 — Object preview

**Purpose:** let the sender verify content, recipient, material, and the finite object before sharing.

**Required:** edit path, honest simulated label, no fake recipient reaction, no confetti, no engagement forecast.

### S-06 — Share and sender completion

**Purpose:** complete the giving action cleanly.

**Required:** simulated share-link action and clear statement that the prototype has not actually delivered data. After completion, the sender can make another object or leave; they cannot monitor the receiver.

### R-01 — Receiver arrival

**Purpose:** reduce safety and interpretation ambiguity before opening.

**Required:** known sender name, neutral statement that they made a private keepsake, open action, and leave/defer action.

**Prohibited:** urgent animation, alarm language, unknown sender reveal, emotional content preview, read-state transmission, auto-play.

### R-02 — Seal / reveal

**Purpose:** turn opening into an intentional boundary between notification and object.

**Behaviour:** one concise reveal. Interface chrome recedes as the object becomes the screen. A wax seal, custom mark, or firefly can make the transition legible if it improves understanding.

**Motion:** must have a reduced-motion equivalent that opens directly.

### R-03 — Keepsake

**Purpose:** let the receiver absorb the act without performing a public reaction.

**Content order:** sender-authored words first; one optional material second. System controls remain visually subordinate.

**No reply condition:** there is no reply field. Whether explicit no-reply copy appears is a test variant, not a universal truth.

### R-04 — Receiver ending

**Purpose:** make receiver ownership concrete.

- **Keep:** save to the local prototype shelf; do not notify the sender.
- **Close:** return to the arrival or previous surface without saving; do not notify the sender.
- **Discard:** show a plain confirmation, then remove locally; do not notify the sender.

No option is styled as the morally correct response.

### R-05 — Revisit shelf

**Purpose:** test whether private persistence is valued.

**Rules:** no sender analytics, sorting by friendship health, frequency, streak, or value. The object remains visually distinct. The empty state is calm and does not ask the receiver to solicit more messages.

### R-06 — Invalid, expired, or unsafe link

**Purpose:** fail without exposing private content.

**Required:** plain explanation, no sender or message leakage beyond safe metadata, and an exit. Expiry policy remains open for a real system.

## 14. Interaction and copy rules

### 14.1 Voice

- Plain, warm, specific, and calm.
- No therapy language, moral instruction, or universal claim about friendship.
- No “spread kindness,” “make someone’s day,” “your friend needs you,” or engagement-style calls to action.
- No copy that shames silence or celebrates volume.
- No gender stereotypes or raw slur language from the transcripts.

### 14.2 System copy examples

These are provisional and may be rewritten by the team:

- Entry: “make something for one person”
- Reason field: “what made you think of them?”
- Material step: “one thing that carries the memory”
- Arrival: “[Name] made something private for you.”
- Defer: “open another time”
- Ending: “keep”, “close”, “discard”

Avoid stating “no pressure” as a magic solution. Structure must remove the pressure-producing affordances first. An explicit closure line can be tested separately.

### 14.3 Destructive copy

Discard confirmation is direct and neutral: what will be removed, whether it is recoverable in this prototype, and that the sender will not be told. No guilt or warning colour is used beyond what clarity requires.

## 15. Visual and material specification

### 15.1 Visual purpose

The style must make the experience feel authored, quiet, tactile, and specific without copying the supplied `grug` brand or turning roughness into decoration.

### 15.2 Source anchors

- `prototype/references/grug-handdrawn-style-reference.png`
- `prototype/references/handwriting-type-moodboard.png`
- `prototype/references/book-cover-colour-palettes.png`
- Cecelia’s forthcoming hand-drawn asset set

These references guide visual language only. Do not copy their wordmark, mascot, copy, sunrise, phone layout, or exact composition.

### 15.3 Typography

- Use the exact local `uglyhandwriting.ttf` for the current prototype’s display type, primary object text, and short interface labels.
- Maintain legibility: body text should not fall below a comfortable mobile reading size, letterforms must not clip, and critical disclosures may use a restrained system fallback if the handwriting becomes unreadable.
- No generic neo-grotesk “AI startup” treatment, all-caps label stacks, or slogan fragments.

### 15.4 Colour

Current candidate palette:

- paper `#F2EAD5`
- sage `#9AA99B`
- rust `#9E5A24`
- olive `#73744E`
- deep blue `#183B49`
- oxblood `#651D25`
- amber `#CF842B`
- tan `#CDBB9D`
- black ink as the constant

Use one dominant field plus at most one supporting colour per state/object. Colour should feel like worn book covers, not a rainbow product system.

Keep the surrounding product shell sparse and comparatively neutral so the object can carry colour without the interface competing with it. Object colour tokens must be swappable in code. A preset/custom colour picker was proposed in the transcripts, but it is not a P0 control until testing shows personal colour choice adds authorship rather than editor complexity.

### 15.5 Layout

- Mobile-first, quiet, and object-led.
- One main decision per screen.
- Large areas of intentional space.
- A few strong silhouettes instead of repeated SaaS cards.
- Irregularity comes from hand-made marks and optical placement, not broken alignment.
- The receiver’s object can take over the whole screen; navigation chrome disappears during the reveal/experience.
- Avoid excessive pills, gradients, glass panels, tiny uppercase metadata, equal-weight card grids, and “three feature” layouts.

### 15.6 Hand-drawn assets

Every generic icon in the exploratory build is temporary. Cecelia’s first useful asset pass should cover:

- wordmark or neutral name lock-up;
- new-object mark;
- back/close, play/pause/replay, image, microphone, edit, share, keep, discard;
- sealed and opened object states;
- one wax seal and one custom stamp frame;
- one quiet firefly arrival mark if the team keeps that branch;
- one shelf/container detail and two small dividers/scribbles;
- reduced-motion/static alternatives.

The firefly must signal arrival or attention. The wax seal must signal unopened/opened state. If either is merely cute, remove it.

### 15.7 Motion and sound

- Motion explains arrival, opening, transfer, or ownership.
- The reveal is brief, cancellable, and never blocks access.
- No auto-playing audio.
- Reduced-motion mode replaces movement with an immediate state change.
- No confetti, floating particles, looping ambient motion, or decorative parallax unless a later test shows a clear interaction benefit.

### 15.8 Asset architecture

Visual assets must be swappable without changing flow logic. Brand selection cannot block functional testing. Firefly, seal, stamp, courier, and shelf treatments live behind replaceable asset slots rather than hard-coded layout assumptions.

## 16. Privacy, consent, and trust

### 16.1 Prototype requirements

- Use only fictional/sample content unless a participant explicitly consents.
- Label all simulated delivery, identity, storage, and deletion.
- Do not collect contacts, messages, media, or analytics outside the local prototype.
- Do not imply that the sender can see receiver behaviour.
- Do not use a real judge’s personal information in sample content.

### 16.2 Requirements before a real service

- unguessable link tokens and no personal data in the URL;
- no search indexing or rich-link preview leakage of intimate content;
- clear forwarding risk and receiver identity model;
- encryption in transit and at rest;
- receiver-controlled deletion and export;
- sender revocation rules that do not contradict receiver ownership;
- abuse, blocking, and unwanted-contact handling;
- media retention, consent, and copyright rules;
- clear response when a link is invalid, expired, or opened by the wrong person.

### 16.3 Emotional safety

The experience cannot guarantee that a message will not be read as crisis, romance, farewell, or obligation. Test those interpretations directly. If a participant reads a message as a possible safety concern, stop the research task, clarify that the content is part of a consented test, and follow the agreed human safety protocol.

## 17. Accessibility and inclusion

- All primary controls are at least 44 × 44 CSS pixels.
- Keyboard navigation, visible focus, logical focus order, and labelled controls are required.
- Every photo has a useful description; every voice/video item has a transcript or equivalent text.
- Colour is never the only state indicator.
- Contrast must meet WCAG AA for functional text and controls.
- Handwriting must remain readable at the intended size; aesthetic roughness cannot override comprehension.
- Reduced motion provides the full journey without animation.
- Audio never auto-plays and playback state is announced accessibly.
- The mobile layout handles text resizing, long names, long content, and narrow screens without clipping.
- The pitch demo has a spoken/printed/no-device route equal to the QR route.
- Cultural and communication-style variation is treated as real variation, not resistance to be corrected.

## 18. Empty, error, and recovery states

| State | Required response |
| --- | --- |
| Empty shelf | Explain the space without guilt or a prompt to solicit messages |
| Blank person/reason | Identify the missing field and keep the draft |
| Blank body | Do not allow completion; explain that the sender’s words are the core |
| No material | Continue normally; text-only is complete |
| Unsupported photo/audio | Offer removal or accessible fallback; preserve body text |
| Playback failure | Show transcript and retry; never trap the receiver |
| Share failure | State that nothing was sent; preserve the full draft and preview |
| Accidental back/close | Return safely with local state intact |
| Invalid/expired link | Explain without exposing content; provide exit |
| Unknown/suspicious sender | Do not reveal content automatically; provide safe exit/report path in a real service |
| Receiver inactivity | Do nothing; no reminders or sender signal |
| Discard selected | Confirm; allow cancel; remove locally; never notify sender |
| Reduced motion | Open directly into the same content and ending |

## 19. Experiment specification

### 19.1 Minimum participant set

Recruit 6–8 consented friendship pairs if time allows. Recruit across genders and friendship rhythms. Do not describe them as representative. If fewer pairs are available, report the exact number and treat findings as directional.

### 19.2 Conditions

Hold the specific message as constant as ethically possible and compare:

1. ordinary text/DM control;
2. ordinary text with explicit “no reply needed” language;
3. finite digital object with structural closure and no explicit no-reply claim;
4. finite digital object with structural closure plus explicit closure copy;
5. optional reference condition: voice note or physical/crafted note.

The prototype only needs to implement conditions 3 and 4 directly; the other controls can use existing tools or static matched stimuli.

### 19.3 Sender measures

- predicted receiver warmth;
- predicted awkwardness, alarm, romantic reading, or reply pressure;
- confidence that the message is specific and self-authored;
- completion time and abandonment point;
- whether the sender would genuinely use the channel again;
- preferred existing alternative and why.

### 19.4 Receiver measures

- actual warmth and appreciation;
- interpretation: ordinary care, crisis, farewell, romance, prompted task, or obligation;
- perceived need to reply quickly or match effort;
- sense of control over opening and ending;
- authenticity/provenance judgment;
- desire to keep or revisit;
- whether persistence feels comforting, neutral, or invasive;
- preferred existing alternative and why.

### 19.5 Observation rules

- Collect sender predictions before delivery and receiver reports privately afterward.
- Do not let the sender watch the receiver open it.
- Do not infer success from smiles, replies, scans, applause, or time spent.
- Record negative and “would not use” responses without persuasion.
- Do not fabricate or backfill missing responses.
- Separate usability defects from rejection of the core social contract.

### 19.6 Decision gate after testing

The direction may advance only if the team can show which mechanism changed the experience: object form, structural closure, explicit copy, authorship, media, or something else. If the result is only “people liked the visuals,” return to the problem.

## 20. Prototype acceptance criteria

### Functional

- **AC-F01:** A sender cannot finish without a named person, specific reason, and self-authored body.
- **AC-F02:** Text-only completion works.
- **AC-F03:** An object contains at most one selected supporting material.
- **AC-F04:** Voice/photo states are interactive or explicitly labelled as simulated.
- **AC-F05:** Preview, edit, share attempt, share failure, and sender completion have distinct states.
- **AC-F06:** The sender never sees receiver activity.
- **AC-F07:** The receiver can identify the sender before opening.
- **AC-F08:** Defer/leave works without a returned signal.
- **AC-F09:** Keep, close, and discard are distinct.
- **AC-F10:** Discard requires confirmation and can be cancelled.
- **AC-F11:** Close does not silently save.
- **AC-F12:** No core control is visually present but inert.

### Social contract

- **AC-S01:** No chat bubbles, reply field, typing state, read receipt, reaction count, or streak appears.
- **AC-S02:** Receiver inactivity is treated as a complete valid outcome.
- **AC-S03:** The object can be understood as finite without reading a product manifesto.
- **AC-S04:** No generated intimate content or assigned-recipient prompt appears.
- **AC-S05:** No option pressures the receiver to validate the sender.

### Accessibility

- **AC-A01:** Primary targets are at least 44 × 44 CSS pixels.
- **AC-A02:** Keyboard and visible-focus paths cover the whole core journey.
- **AC-A03:** Photo and voice examples have equivalent text.
- **AC-A04:** Reduced motion preserves every state and action.
- **AC-A05:** No content clips at 320 px width or with long names/body text.
- **AC-A06:** Colour is not the only state signal and functional contrast passes WCAG AA.

### Visual authenticity

- **AC-V01:** Exact Ugly Handwriting is loaded from the local font asset.
- **AC-V02:** Each screen has one dominant decision and one dominant colour field.
- **AC-V03:** The receiver object can remove app chrome and become the screen.
- **AC-V04:** Firefly/seal/stamp/shelf assets perform a narrative role or are removed.
- **AC-V05:** Generic library icons are recorded as temporary and replaced by Cecelia’s hand-drawn set before final presentation.
- **AC-V06:** The layout passes the logo-swap test: replacing the name does not make it look like a generic journaling, card, or notes app.
- **AC-V07:** The interface passes a subtraction pass: decorative containers, labels, and repeated cards not needed for comprehension are removed.

### Evidence and demo truth

- **AC-E01:** Simulated behaviour is labelled.
- **AC-E02:** No fabricated survey, quote, statistic, persona, or impact result appears.
- **AC-E03:** A QR demo is optional and has a no-device equivalent.
- **AC-E04:** Judge/audience reaction is not reported as validation.
- **AC-E05:** Any outcome claim links to either external research or an exact project test record with limitations.

## 21. Open decisions, in order

### Blocker before claiming product fit

1. Which exact sender–receiver pair/context experiences the strongest repeatable problem?
2. Is the main loss withheld expression, misinterpretation, reply debt, or lack of durable memory?
3. Does structural closure work without explicit “no reply” copy?
4. Does the receiver want a private archive, temporary object, or choice at receipt?
5. What digital capability is necessary rather than decorative?

### Blocker before final interaction design

6. Does any acknowledgement exist, and who can see it?
7. What happens to sender control after transfer?
8. Which single object metaphor best communicates the contract?
9. Is the first optional material photo, voice, or receiver-selected?
10. How are forwarding, expiry, deletion, and wrong-recipient access handled?

### Blocker before final branding

11. Is the firefly retained as a courier/arrival mark?
12. Is a wax seal the right state metaphor or a superficial imitation of paper?
13. Does “Warm and Fuzzies” survive naming review, competitor search, and team confirmation?
14. Which Cecelia marks become product components rather than decoration?
15. Does choosing an object colour or package materially increase authorship, or only add surface customisation?

The deck remains brand-free and structurally black/white until these product decisions settle. Prototype branding is a candidate visual world, not automatic presentation branding.

## 22. Contradictions resolved for this prototype

| Transcript tension | Prototype resolution |
| --- | --- |
| “Text is meaningless” versus text can be meaningful | Do not demean text; test whether turn-based context changes interpretation |
| Physical letter as gold standard versus digital can be better | No superiority claim; test digital-specific capabilities against controls |
| Letter versus scrapbook versus object | Use one finite keepsake; letter is a writing affordance, not the full category |
| Instant delivery versus bottle-style delay | No forced delay in v0; arrival ritual is immediate and non-blocking |
| Daily random appreciation versus self-authored provenance | No assignment or reminder; sender initiates from a real reason |
| Male friendship emphasis versus broad friendship/family | Use a neutral friendship scenario; masculinity remains a research lens |
| “No reply” as the solution versus possible backfire | Structural closure is the main condition; explicit copy is a separate test variant |
| Archive as ownership versus possible burden | Keep/close/discard are receiver-controlled; shelf is removable and tested |
| Multiple couriers/themes versus coherent first experience | One postal-object expression; firefly/wax seal remain replaceable assets |
| Selectable packages and custom colours versus a calm first build | Keep tokens and assets swappable, but expose one coherent object treatment in v0 |
| Brand lock before screens versus function first | Flow logic is canonical; all branded assets are swappable |
| AI research replacing human research | AI handles desk research and synthesis; human behaviour claims require real participants |

## 25. Build handoff

Before changing the prototype:

1. map every visible screen and state to an item in sections 9–12;
2. record any deviation as either a deliberate experiment or an unresolved defect;
3. keep all visual assets replaceable;
4. use realistic but fictional content;
5. verify the sender and receiver paths independently;
6. run judge, challenge, authenticity, accessibility, and visual-comparison review on the actual render;
7. do not present the result as a validated product.

The immediate build target is therefore narrow: **one complete, honest, accessible sender-to-receiver keepsake journey whose social contract is more visible than its decoration.**

The live screen/state mapping and deliberate deferrals are recorded in [`prototype/IMPLEMENTATION_NOTES.md`](../prototype/IMPLEMENTATION_NOTES.md).
