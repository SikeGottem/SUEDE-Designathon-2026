# Creation mechanism tests

Status: **exploratory — test first, not selected product direction**.

The current maker is a capable scrapbook editor, but it starts after the hardest work. It assumes the sender already knows which memory matters, what is safe to say, and how to make the appreciation specific. More paper styles, stickers, carriers, or tone controls will not solve that bottleneck.

The actual creation job appears to be:

1. Notice that a person matters now.
2. Retrieve one concrete moment or repeated behaviour.
3. Identify what that moment revealed about them.
4. Decide what feels true and safe to share.
5. Express it in the sender's own language.
6. Only then compose the receiver-facing object.

This implies two deliberately different spaces: a private **backstage** for remembering and a receiver-facing **frontstage** for making. Nothing from backstage should transfer automatically.

## Three mechanisms worth prototyping first

### 1. Backstage → Frontstage

- Before the blank paper, offer a private scratch area labelled as unseen by the receiver.
- Let the sender jot fragments, contradictions, names, images, or voice notes without arranging them.
- The sender manually carries only chosen fragments onto the final paper.
- Why test it: it lowers the pressure to produce a polished message immediately while preserving consent and authorship.
- Main risk: it could feel like an extra form or imply that private drafts are stored.

### 2. Proof before praise

- Ask one small question at a time, never a long questionnaire:
  - What happened?
  - What did they actually do or say?
  - What did that show you about them?
  - Why did it matter that day?
- Show the sender's exact answers as loose scraps; they select, cross out, combine, or discard them.
- Do not generate a finished compliment or rewrite their voice.
- Why test it: it moves appreciation from generic traits toward observable evidence.
- Main risk: the sequence could feel therapeutic, interrogative, or too slow for an ordinary-day thought.

### 3. Voice-to-Ink

- Give the sender 45 seconds to speak messily about the person or moment.
- Transcribe only exact uttered clauses into movable pieces.
- Let the sender cut, reorder, cross out, and keep their own phrasing; no AI paraphrase button.
- Why test it: speaking may retrieve detail faster than facing a blank page while keeping a recognisable voice.
- Main risk: privacy, transcription error, accessibility, and the feeling of being recorded.

## Further mechanism candidates

- **Memory contact sheet:** show prompts based on places, times, shared objects, recurring jokes, or “the last time they made something easier,” then let the sender open only one thread.
- **Noticing pocket:** during ordinary life, privately collect tiny observations without assigning them to a future message; later choose one as the seed.
- **Curator's label:** compose around one artifact—photo, voice fragment, screenshot, song, or doodle—and answer only “why this belongs to them.”
- **One true thing:** temporarily limit the sender to one specific sentence before any decoration appears.
- **Unsendable first pass:** explicitly invite the clumsy, over-intense, or incomplete version, then let the sender decide what crosses into the final object.
- **Receiver lens:** before giving, privately ask whether any line demands reassurance, repayment, public disclosure, or a reply; offer deletion, not automated rewriting.
- **Care register:** let the sender choose whether this relationship communicates through teasing, quiet acts, direct words, shared media, or practical help; use the choice only to vary prompts, never to fake their tone.
- **Three physical scraps:** require one moment, one observed quality, and one effect on the sender; allow any scrap to be removed so the result need not become a formula.

## Small tests before implementation

1. **Blank page vs private backstage:** give matched participants the same appreciation task and compare time to first specific detail, not visual completion.
2. **Prompt specificity test:** compare generic prompts with `what happened / what did they do / why did it matter`; stop if the sequence produces canned answers.
3. **Wizard-of-Oz Voice-to-Ink:** manually transcribe exact clauses, then observe whether senders recognise the result as their voice and how much they edit.
4. **Receiver blind read:** compare a current freeform object with a retrieval-assisted object; ask which feels more specific, believable, pressured, and recognisably from that person.

Useful signals are concrete memories surfaced, sender-recognised language, low time to first true fragment, voluntary deletion, receiver-perceived specificity, and no increased pressure to reply. Visual polish, word count, and number of materials are not success measures.

## Guardrail

Do not add more decorative choices or AI-written praise before one retrieval mechanism beats the current blank creator. The product should help the sender remember and select; it should not manufacture intimacy on their behalf.
