# Friendship appreciation: problem, medium, and provisional ICP

**Audience:** SUEDE Designathon 2026 team
**Research date:** 4 September 2026
**Geographic emphasis:** Australia, with international behavioural evidence kept within its original context
**Status:** deep desk research; not primary validation
**Decision boundary:** this report can narrow hypotheses, recruitment, controls, and presentation language. It cannot establish local prevalence, demand, usability, emotional impact, or the winning medium.

## Direct answer

The strongest defensible problem is not that young people are lonely, that people no longer value friends, or that text has destroyed intimacy. Current Australian evidence contradicts those broad stories.

The more credible opportunity is narrower:

> A person can already have a specific appreciative thought about a close friend yet hold it back because they predict awkwardness, relational misreading, or an unwanted conversational obligation. The design question is whether a self-authored, finite receiving experience can help that thought land clearly without scripting intimacy or creating reply debt.

Published studies support the **sender prediction gap** and show that communication form can change interpretation. They do not prove that the proposed digital keepsake is wanted or better than chat, voice, a physical letter, or an act of care. Direct thanks is also culturally and relationally marked; sometimes it can imply that ordinary friendship duties were exceeded and therefore signal distance.

The best provisional recruitment hypothesis is a **transition-separated but still-close friendship pair**, roughly ages 22–34, where one person recently held back a concrete ordinary-day expression because chat felt too casual or too conversational. This is an interview and test segment, not a validated ideal customer profile or market claim.

## Research questions

1. Is ordinary-day appreciation actually withheld, and what prediction stops it?
2. Does the receiver independently experience the same ambiguity or reply pressure?
3. What does a distinct container change when semantic content is held constant?
4. Which medium best balances authorship, warmth, privacy, effort, accessibility, ownership, and friction?
5. Who experiences the tension strongly enough to prefer a new format over existing communication?
6. Which claims are safe for the live pitch, which belong in the appendix, and which must remain hypotheses?

## Method

- Searched peer-reviewed behavioural, communication, HCI, and consumer research for gratitude expression, reaching out, reciprocity, medium effects, voice, text, handwriting, physical/digital ownership, delayed messaging, and mementos.
- Prioritised original papers, official publisher pages, open full texts, Australian government statistics, and official competitor/product pages.
- Searched for disconfirming evidence rather than ranking formats from intuition.
- Separated exact-content comparisons from studies where participants adapted their message to the medium.
- Separated evidence about gratitude, support, favours, gifts, reconnection, and ordinary friendship rather than treating them as interchangeable.
- Audited current alternatives using vendor-stated capabilities only; vendor pages do not prove adoption or outcomes.
- Used all current team transcripts as qualitative design inputs, not population evidence.

## 1. Problem evidence

### 1.1 Sender miscalibration is the strongest foundation

Kumar and Epley's real gratitude-letter experiments found that writers underestimated recipients' positive mood and perceived warmth/competence, while overestimating awkwardness. In one experiment, 107 MBA students wrote letters and 80 recipients responded; in a preregistered experiment, 127 wrote and 78 recipients responded. The result supports a prediction gap, but incomplete recipient response can select for happier reactions, and targets included parents, partners, teachers, and friends. [Kumar & Epley, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797618772506)

A separate series found that initiators often underestimate how much a person appreciates being contacted, including lower-contact ties. An independent preregistered scenario replication with 742 participants found a small prediction gap. That does not mean dormant friends value outreach more than close friends; it means surprise can enlarge the initiator's forecasting error. [Liu et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000402), [independent replication](https://replications.clearerthinking.org/replication-2023jpsp124-4/)

**Presentation-safe:** “In US gratitude-letter studies, writers underestimated recipients' positive reactions and overestimated how awkward the message would feel.”

**Not safe:** “Everyone secretly wants an appreciation message.”

### 1.2 Felt appreciation and explicit thanks are not identical

Researchers coded 1,057 naturally occurring request sequences across eight languages; 928 requests were fulfilled, yet explicit gratitude followed only 51 fulfilments. The authors did not interpret silence as an absence of gratitude: cooperation can rely on tacit rights, duties, and reciprocity. The context was fulfilled requests, not unsolicited friendship appreciation. [Floyd et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5990755/)

**Design consequence:** do not moralise people as ungrateful or force verbal expression to replace humour, presence, practical help, shared activity, or culturally normal tacit care.

### 1.3 Direct gratitude is not culturally neutral

Across seven US/China studies, direct thanks could signal that relationship expectations had been exceeded. Chinese participants were less likely than US participants to express direct gratitude to close others, but not distant others; duties expected within close relationships helped explain the pattern. This challenges the assumption that explicitness always communicates intimacy. It does not establish an Australian effect. [Yu & Chaudhry, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000435), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37796591/)

**Presentation-safe:** “Direct thanks is not culturally neutral; in some close relationships it can make ordinary mutual care sound exceptional.”

**Design consequence:** provide calibrated framing and multiple expressive modes; never position more emotional disclosure as universally healthier.

### 1.4 Prompts can weaken provenance

Across ten studies, gratitude prompted by a reminder was judged less genuine and the expresser less likeable than spontaneous gratitude, including receiver judgments. This does not prove every prompt is harmful. It makes daily assignments, random friend prompts, streaks, and automated reminders consequential rather than neutral. [Wang, Chaudhry & Koch, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000442), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38010756/)

**Design consequence:** start only when the sender already has a person or moment in mind. If composition cues exist, they help articulate the sender's reason; they must not assign a person or generate the sentiment.

### 1.5 Reply pressure is plausible but not yet gratitude-specific

Friendship research links mobile-maintenance expectations with overdependence and entrapment: guilt or pressure to respond. Availability-pressure experiments and diaries likewise connect high availability pressure with reduced autonomy and worse affect. Neither directly tests a heartfelt appreciation message. [Hall & Baym, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1177/1461444811415047), [Halfmann & Rieger, 2019](https://academic.oup.com/jcmc/article/24/4/165/5487068)

Research on indebtedness distinguishes altruistic intent, which conveys care and gratitude, from strategic intent, which suggests expected future reciprocity and creates obligation. This work concerns favours, not appreciation artifacts, but it supports testing whether implied return expectations change reception. [Gao et al., 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10762097/)

**Presentation-safe:** “Messaging expectations can reduce autonomy, so reply pressure is a design risk worth testing.”

**Not safe:** “Receiving appreciation inherently makes people indebted.”

## 2. Australian context

### 2.1 Do not pitch a generational connection crisis

In the ABS 2025 General Social Survey, 58.7% of Australians aged 15–24 and 53.9% aged 25–34 reported at least weekly face-to-face contact with family or friends outside their household. The lowest age group was 35–44 at 43.1%. Most Australians could get help when needed, had someone to confide in, and could obtain crisis support. [ABS General Social Survey 2025](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/people-and-communities/general-social-survey-summary-results-australia/2025)

AIHW's HILDA synthesis estimated loneliness among 15% of Australians in 2024, with the highest age-group rate among 35–44s rather than the proposed youth audience. It also reports a separate social-isolation measure. [AIHW social isolation and loneliness](https://www.aihw.gov.au/mental-health/topic-areas/health-wellbeing/social-isolation-and-loneliness)

**Pitch implication:** this is not a loneliness cure or a story about a broken generation. It is a bounded communication and interpretation problem among people who already value a relationship.

### 2.2 Digital familiarity supports feasibility, not demand

ACMA reports that 94% of Australian adults aged 18–34 used apps for messages, calls, or video calls in 2025; traditional SMS use was 96%, from a survey base of 3,543 adults. The audience is digitally familiar. That makes another app harder to justify, not automatically easier to adopt. [ACMA, *How we communicate*](https://www.acma.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-02/Trends%20and%20developments%20in%20telecommunications%202024-25.pdf)

**Design consequence:** test an accountless receiver link sent through an existing channel before building a two-sided network.

### 2.3 Transitions are a useful recruitment cue

Australians aged 18–40 represented 55% of interstate movers in 2021 while comprising 32% of the population. This supports recruitment around relocation, graduation, and work transitions. It does not show that movers suppress appreciation or want a keepsake. [ABS interstate migration analysis](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/detailed-methodology-information/information-papers/2021-census-update-net-interstate-migration-model)

Friendships are important to young Australians, and most report available support. That supports the importance of the relationship without proving a deficit. [AIHW peer relationships](https://www.aihw.gov.au/reports/children-youth/peer-relationships)

## 3. Medium effects: no universal hierarchy

### 3.1 Voice can add social presence

In randomized live-conversation studies, reconnecting by phone produced stronger bonding than email without greater awkwardness; in stranger conversations, audio and audiovisual conditions produced more connection than synchronous text, while video did not outperform audio. The wording could adapt by medium, and this was not asynchronous voice-note use. [Kumar & Epley, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000962)

When verbal content was held constant in other experiments, hearing rather than reading could increase trust or perceived mental capacity. Those tasks measured hidden-action trust and reactions to polarizing statements, not friendship sincerity. [Babutsidze, Hanaki & Zylbersztejn, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/ecin.12998), [Schroeder, Kardas & Epley, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617713798)

**Presentation-safe:** “Voice cues can change connection, trust, and how fully a sender is perceived, but video is not automatically better than audio.”

### 3.2 Text has advantages the concept must preserve

Text can improve precision and disclosure in sensitive survey responses, and email's editability can support polite construction of difficult requests. In a romantic-message study, senders produced more positive language and arousal in email than voicemail for romantic content. These contexts are not direct evidence about recipients of friendship appreciation, but they decisively reject “text is emotionally shallow” as a general claim. [Schober et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0128337), [Duthler, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1083-6101.2006.00024.x), [Wells & Dennis, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2015.07.036)

Asynchronous voice notes are also conditional: a preregistered workplace experiment found they reduced negative interpretation when vocal cues resolved ambiguity, but worsened it for some groups when cues did not clarify the message. [Cunningham & Fleming, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbr.2026.101020)

**Design consequence:** retain private pacing, editing, captions/transcripts, and a written core. Offer voice as a material, not the “more authentic” default.

### 3.3 Handwriting can signal effort, but is not proof of care

Handwritten service messages increased perceived warmth through effort and psychological closeness in one field and three lab studies, but the benefit disappeared under poor core service quality. A small study with eight romantic couples found that handwriting and physical rendering could convey identity, effort, commitment, sincerity, and intimacy while remaining inconvenient for routine talk. [Ren, Xia & Du, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1108/JSM-04-2017-0133), [Kim, Son & Nam, 2019](https://doi.org/10.15187/adr.2019.05.32.2.45)

**Design consequence:** visible authorship matters more than fake handwriting. The prototype may use a hand-drawn visual system, but it must not imply the sender physically wrote words they typed or let AI impersonate their voice.

### 3.4 Physicality adds ownership, not automatic superiority

Across matched physical/digital goods, participants often valued physical versions more, with psychological ownership mediating part of the difference. The effect depended on expected ownership, identity relevance, and perceived control. Home studies also found that digital mementos were less integrated into everyday environments and could feel less possessed when copied or platform-dependent. [Atasoy & Morewedge, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucx102), [Petrelli & Whittaker, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00779-009-0279-7), [Odom et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1145/2207676.2207789)

An exact email-versus-print study found no evidence that print alone improved recipient ratings; reply behaviour was explained by response effort. Printed internal mail is not a handwritten posted letter, but the finding blocks a simplistic physical-goodness story. [Hill & Monk, 2000](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0953-5438(00)00040-0)

**Design consequence:** digital ownership must be made visible through durable access, receiver control, clear transfer, privacy, and revisitation. It cannot be assumed from a card-like appearance.

### 3.5 Delay is a cue, not value by itself

A six-week deployment of delayed photo/video messages found uses around reflection, memory, and future relationships, alongside vulnerability, regret, and uncertainty. Other work shows that delayed messaging can violate expectations and that timing meaning depends on shared context and message contingency. [Hawkins, Neustaedter & Procyk, 2015](https://graphicsinterface.org/proceedings/gi2015/gi2015-32/), [Tu, Yuan & Wang, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1145/3173574.3173675), [Lew et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcmc/zmy009)

**Design consequence:** do not force slowness, sell delivery speed, or claim delay causes intimacy. If timing exists, the sender and receiver understand and choose its meaning.

### 3.6 Richer is not automatically more memorable

Multimodal pen-and-voice systems improved preference and efficiency in older task-communication studies, while controlled reading/listening studies found no simple universal memory ranking. These do not measure emotional revisitation. [Daly-Jones et al., 1997](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0953-5438(96)01041-7), [Rogowsky, Calhoun & Tallal, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/2158244016669550)

**Design consequence:** the first prototype uses a written core plus one supporting medium. Creative abundance is a later hypothesis, not proof.

## 4. Provisional ideal customer profile

### Recommended recruitment hypothesis

> An Australian aged roughly 22–34 who still values a close friend after relocation, graduation, work change, or another transition; has recently held back one specific ordinary-day expression of appreciation because a normal chat made it feel disproportionately intense or like the start of a conversation; and wants the friend to receive it without owing an immediate or equally meaningful reply.

Receiver condition:

> The friend also regards the relationship as intact, welcomes direct care, and wants control over when to open, whether to keep, whether to acknowledge, and whether any later conversation occurs.

This segment is useful because it combines a concrete trigger, relationship history, current workaround, and receiving tension. Age is secondary; the behaviour is the screening criterion.

### Job, context, and failure moment

- **Trigger:** a photo, song, old joke, remembered act, or ordinary observation makes one friend salient.
- **Job:** let that friend know one precise thing they mean without promising a live catch-up or demanding emotional symmetry.
- **Current workaround:** send a meme or reaction, write a short DM, say “we should catch up,” wait for a birthday, do a practical favour, use a voice note, or say nothing.
- **Failure moment:** the sender edits the message down, adds humour to defuse it, postpones it to an occasion, or abandons it because the receiver's likely interpretation is unclear.
- **Desired receiver outcome:** “I can take this in privately, understand why it arrived, keep it if I want, and choose my own ending.”
- **Desired sender outcome:** “I can transfer the thought without monitoring whether they opened it or requiring a matching response.”

### Candidate segments compared

| Candidate | Why it may fit | Main risk | Current use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Transition-separated, still-close pair, 22–34 | Mobility, digital familiarity, and lower-contact triggers make recruitment concrete | Distance does not prove suppressed appreciation; chat may be sufficient | **Lead recruitment hypothesis** |
| Hesitant sender in an emotionally indirect friendship, 18–29 | Closest to the gratitude prediction gap and the unwritten rule | Could be personal style rather than a shared norm; prevalence unknown | Include as positive cases |
| Low-capacity receiver in an intact low-contact friendship, 28–44 | Strong receiver-first test of warmth without a conversation task | “No reply” can sound cold or alarming; low capacity is not keepsake demand | Include as adjacent/negative cases |
| Male friendship pair with a narrow care register, 18–30 | Some masculinity norms may make tenderness more socially costly | High stereotype risk; most Australian men report connection; gender is not mechanism | Research branch only |

### Exclusions

- estranged relationships, unresolved conflict, or a stated no-contact boundary;
- crisis messages, apologies, confessions, bereavement, farewell, or romantic signalling;
- people who already exchange direct appreciation comfortably;
- receivers who want frequent reciprocal conversation;
- recipients who dislike sentimental artifacts or do not want a relationship reopened;
- workplace recognition, family legacy, group milestone cards, and public praise;
- people whose preferred care is practical action and who do not want it translated into words.

### Evidence status

- **Supported separately:** high digital familiarity, friendship importance, mobility among younger adults, sender prediction error, and the contextual effects of communication form.
- **Not supported as one segment:** no dataset estimates intact friendship + ordinary-day appreciation withheld + interpretation/reply concern + preference for a separate artifact.
- **Not available:** defensible market size, willingness to pay, acquisition channel, retention, or repeat frequency.
- **Use in presentation:** label this as “who we are testing first,” not “our ICP” as if validated.

### Competitive conclusion

The market is not empty, and “private,” “thinking of you,” “keepsake,” “no reply required,” “slow,” and “multimedia card” are not differentiators alone.

The concept must prove a more precise mechanism:

> The receiver controls the boundary of a deliberately transferred object, while the sender loses the familiar tracking and conversational machinery that turns care into social accounting.

Even that mechanism is a hypothesis until tested. It may create sender anxiety or receiver alarm.

## 6. Product requirements derived from evidence

### Must be present in the first testable concept

- self-chosen person and reason; no assigned friend or reminder;
- self-authored written core; no AI-written intimate text;
- one finite object rather than a message stream;
- one supporting medium at most in the core test;
- known-sender cue and calm, non-urgent arrival framing;
- receiver choice over opening, keeping, closing, and deletion;
- no reply composer, read receipt, typing state, reaction count, streak, ranking, or public trace;
- text alternative for audio, reduced-motion route, visible labels, large targets, and keyboard access;
- accountless receiver path as the first adoption assumption;
- explicit labeling of simulated identity, delivery, upload, and persistence.

### Candidate, not committed

- private cabinet/archive;
- voice, song, photo, video, drawing, or custom composition;
- chosen opening context such as “when you are home”;
- envelope, bird, bottle, e-invite, playlist/CD, camera-roll, or drawer metaphors;
- handwritten or hand-drawn visual language;
- optional low-effort acknowledgement;
- delayed delivery.

### Reject from the first concept

- daily prompts, random assignments, birthdays as the core trigger, or automated follow-up;
- forced scarcity, artificial delay, paid speeds, or delivery gamification;
- AI-generated sentiments or a chatbot that writes emotional content;
- social feed, public reactions, streaks, reciprocity scores, friend rankings, or engagement metrics;
- male-only, mental-health-cure, or “Gen Z is disconnected” framing;
- a recipient account requirement;
- any claim that physical, voice, handwriting, or rich media is inherently more authentic.

## 7. Presentation claim bank

### Safe main-deck claims

1. **Problem:** “People can hold back sincere appreciation because they predict more awkwardness and less positive reception than recipients report.”
2. **Boundary:** “This is not a loneliness cure and not a claim that friends do not already care.”
3. **Insight:** “A meaningful message has two authors of meaning: the person who sends it and the person who decides what it asks of them.”
4. **Design hypothesis:** “We are testing whether a finite, receiver-controlled object can be complete when received rather than behave like the first turn of a chat.”
5. **Medium honesty:** “Voice, text, handwriting, physical form, and delay each change cues and trade-offs; no format is universally best.”
6. **Authorship:** “AI accelerated research and prototyping. People wrote the intimate content, selected the direction, redrew the visual system, and tested the outcome.”

### Useful numbers, with caveats

- Gratitude-letter experiments: writers underestimated positive reception and overestimated awkwardness; report the study context and incomplete recipient response rather than presenting a universal percentage. [Kumar & Epley](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797618772506)
- Weekly face-to-face contact in Australia in 2025: 58.7% for ages 15–24, 53.9% for 25–34, and 43.1% for 35–44. These numbers contradict a youth-disconnection story. [ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/people-and-communities/general-social-survey-summary-results-australia/2025)
- Communication-app use among Australian 18–34s: 94%; SMS: 96%. This establishes familiarity and a strong incumbent, not demand. [ACMA](https://www.acma.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-02/Trends%20and%20developments%20in%20telecommunications%202024-25.pdf)
- Australians aged 18–40 accounted for 55% of interstate movers in 2021 while representing 32% of the population. Use only to justify transition-focused recruitment. [ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/detailed-methodology-information/information-papers/2021-census-update-net-interstate-migration-model)

### Counterevidence for the appendix

- Younger Australians report relatively high face-to-face contact and support.
- Text can enable privacy, precision, disclosure, and careful construction.
- Direct gratitude can sometimes signal relational distance.
- Reciprocity is a valued feature of friendship, not automatically a harmful debt.
- Voice notes can worsen ambiguity when vocal cues do not clarify intent.
- Print alone did not improve recipient ratings in an exact-content comparison.
- Physical goods often feel more owned; digital objects face a real possession/control disadvantage.
- Existing products already claim pressure-free, private, no-reply care.
- Delay can create vulnerability and uncertainty rather than intimacy.
- A normal message or non-digital act may win.

### Claims to prohibit

- “Gen Z has forgotten how to connect.”
- “Men cannot tell their friends they care.”
- “People do not appreciate their friends enough.”
- “Text is shallow” or “text is too efficient.”
- “Handwriting is more authentic.”
- “Voice is always warmer.”
- “Physical letters are too difficult.”
- “Digital is the perfect middle.”
- “No reply needed removes pressure.”
- “Rich media makes memories last.”
- “The market has no solution.”
- Any invented prevalence, market-size, willingness-to-pay, emotional-impact, or retention number.

## 8. Minimum primary validation

AI desk research has removed most factual and precedent work. The remaining human work is not duplicate research; it tests the exact combined proposition that no published source currently measures.

### Participants

- Recruit 6–8 friendship pairs matching the transition-separated hypothesis.
- Include negative cases: direct expressive friends, frequent-contact pairs, low-contact pairs where silence is healthy, people who dislike sentimental artifacts, and someone who would not welcome reconnection.
- Interview each person privately before revealing the other's answers.

### Matched conditions

Use identical semantic content and counterbalance order:

1. ordinary chat text with a reply field;
2. asynchronous voice note;
3. designed digital object labeled “no reply needed”;
4. structurally closed digital object with no reply, read state, or follow-up;
5. one finite physical handwritten control.

If time is limited, retain conditions 1, 3, 4, and 5.

### Measure separately

- sender predicted warmth, awkwardness, alarm, sincerity, obligation, and desired response;
- receiver reported warmth, awkwardness, alarm, sincerity, obligation, and desired next action;
- perceived effort and whether that effort feels caring or burdensome;
- preferred opening timing and privacy;
- willingness to keep/discard;
- unaided recall after one to two weeks;
- actual save, reopen, replay, or retain behaviour;
- whether the format added anything beyond the words;
- accessibility or cultural mismatch.

### Stop or pivot rules

- senders cannot recall a real withheld message;
- receivers do not independently identify the same ambiguity or reply tension;
- ordinary chat performs as well or better;
- explicit “no reply needed” increases alarm or distance;
- structural closure creates more sender monitoring anxiety;
- visible creation effort feels like emotional labour or an effort arms race;
- participants want voice, physical action, or an existing tool instead;
- the accountless link creates more trust friction than value.

## 9. Current research verdict

**Verdict: Test first.**

- **Problem direction:** credible enough to test, too narrow and context-sensitive to universalize.
- **Provisional ICP:** useful for recruitment, not established as a market.
- **Digital keepsake:** plausible test stimulus, not the evidence-backed optimum.
- **Strongest mechanism:** receiver control over completion and social accounting, not multimedia or nostalgia.
- **Weakest angle:** evidence quality for the combined Australian sender–receiver condition.
- **Next evidence most likely to change the decision:** whether both sides independently identify the same ordinary-day interpretation/obligation problem and whether structural closure reduces it without increasing alarm.

## Research limitations

- Several studies use students, narrow demographics, hypothetical scenarios, workplace/service contexts, or behaviours adjacent to but not identical with friendship appreciation.
- Effect sizes cannot be transported into an Australian pitch as expected product impact.
- Product pages are first-party descriptions, not independent proof.
- No source found measures the whole proposed condition or establishes market size and willingness to pay.
- The team's transcripts contain genuine reasoning and anecdotes, but they are not independent corroboration.
