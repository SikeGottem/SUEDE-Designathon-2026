# Idea scaffold

Use this with one idea at a time. It is a conversation guide, not paperwork: write short, concrete answers, leave genuinely unknown fields blank, and stop when an answer reveals the next useful test.

Do not use this to make a weak idea look complete. If the person, moment, cause or mechanism is still vague, go back there instead of filling downstream sections.

## How to run it

1. **Capture** the thought before it disappears.
2. **Locate** one person in one real moment, then name the cause beneath the visible problem.
3. **Diverge** into materially different mechanisms before defending the first app-shaped answer.
4. **Pressure-test** the leading mechanism through the challenge questions below.
5. **Choose a next move**, not a winner by default: explore, test, proceed with conditions or stop.
6. When the team is ready to select or build, run the mandatory SUEDE judge review in [TEMPLATES.md](TEMPLATES.md) and record only settled decisions in [DECISIONS.md](DECISIONS.md).

### Conversation rule

Take the sections in the order that helps. A useful 15-minute pass is `Quick capture → Person / moment / cause → Four directions → Red team → Smallest test`. Return for the design handoff only once a direction has earned attention.

### Say it back

Complete this without jargon:

> For **[person]** in **[moment]**, **[intervention]** helps them **[do / feel / avoid a concrete outcome]** because **[cause or insight]**.

If this sounds like it could describe any user, any day or any app, narrow it before continuing.

## Brief-specific lens: map the unwritten rule

Complete this before choosing a medium. An unwritten rule is not just an interesting sentence; it is a pattern maintained by people, signals, incentives and consequences.

### State the rule in lived language

Finish one or both sentences:

> Around here, people like **[person]** are expected to **[behaviour]**, even though nobody formally says so.

> Around here, people like **[person]** do not **[behaviour]**, because **[social or practical consequence]**.

### Locate the rule

- In what exact environment, community, relationship or recurring moment does it operate?
- What visible behaviour would tell an outsider the rule exists?
- What words, silences, gestures, objects, spatial arrangements, interfaces, rituals or sanctions communicate it?
- Who teaches, models, monitors or enforces it?
- Who knows the rule immediately, and who learns it only after making a mistake?
- Who benefits, belongs or gains efficiency because of it?
- Who is burdened, excluded, silenced, confused or made unsafe?
- What exceptions reveal the rule's real boundary?
- Is it genuinely unwritten, or is it a written rule that people selectively enforce?

### Choose the intervention posture

| Posture | What the experience must do | Failure to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reveal | Let people perceive a rule or consequence that normally stays invisible. | Turning the rule into passive information with no felt recognition. |
| Communicate | Help someone recognise, interpret or navigate the rule at the relevant moment. | Teaching conformity without questioning who the rule serves. |
| Question | Create a credible interruption, contrast or reflection that makes the rule debatable. | Producing empty provocation with no path to understanding or agency. |
| Redesign | Change the role, incentive, sequence, environment or ritual that keeps reproducing the rule. | Claiming system change when the prototype only changes a screen. |

- **Chosen posture and why it fits this person and moment:**
- **What participants do, not just what they are told:**
- **What they notice or can do afterwards that was not possible before:**
- **How the experience avoids humiliating, exposing or coercing people affected by the rule:**
- **How we will detect if the intervention strengthens the rule instead of changing it:**

## 2. Find the real problem, not the first symptom

### Person and context

- Who exactly is affected? Do not use a demographic label when a circumstance is what matters.
- What are they doing immediately before the problem appears?
- What are their incentives, fears, time pressure, capability, social context and workarounds?
- What would make this person refuse, ignore or misunderstand help?
- Who else is involved, affected or able to block the outcome?

### Moment and tension

- Describe the scene in enough detail that someone could sketch it:
- What is the user trying to protect, gain, avoid or prove?
- What do they do today?
- What costs them time, dignity, money, attention, safety, belonging or agency?
- What makes the problem recur rather than disappear after one good intention?

### Evidence discipline

- Do not fabricate a quote, statistic, user test or persona to fill a gap.
- Mark simulated prototype behaviour clearly; a convincing demo is not proof of adoption.
- If a claim changes the problem framing, mechanism or pitch, it needs a source or a named test.

## 4. Value: why this person would care

- What does the person gain in the first use?
- What becomes easier, safer, less costly, more possible or more dignified?
- What is the smallest believable outcome, not the ideal future?
- Why would they choose this over doing nothing or using their current workaround?
- What moment would make them come back, tell someone or change a habit?
- What value is for the user, and what value is only for an institution, sponsor or team?
- Could the intervention create a burden, shame, dependency, surveillance or extra work for the person it claims to help?

**Value exchange in one line:**

> The person gives **[attention / data / effort / trust / money]** and gets **[specific near-term outcome]**.

If that exchange is not fair or clear, the idea is not ready for interface design.

## 5. Diverge before choosing

Do not make four feature variants of the same mobile app. Start with the rough idea as a control, then force three different mechanisms.

| Paradigm | Prompt | Proposed mechanism | Where it lives | First benefit | Main risk / unknown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Obvious baseline | What is the most expected app, dashboard, assistant or service answer? Use it only as the control. | | | | |
| 2. In-context intervention | How could the support appear where the behaviour already happens, at the moment it matters? | | | | |
| 3. Non-app alternative | What could a service, ritual, object, environment, policy, incentive, peer network or system change do better? | | | | |
| 4. Inversion / constraint | Remove an assumed feature, actor, screen, account, data source or step. What unexpectedly stronger response appears? | | | | |

### Four extra divergence prompts

Use one or more before converging:

1. **Change the actor:** What if the person cannot do this alone? Who already has leverage at the moment of failure?
2. **Change the timing:** What if the intervention comes before the decision, after the consequence, or only once a week instead of live?
3. **Change the value unit:** What if success is not completion, engagement or information, but confidence, repair, connection, less harm or a changed incentive?
4. **Change the medium:** What if there is no interface, no personal data, no notification, no AI or no new account?

### Keep directions separate

Give each credible direction a temporary name and one-line hypothesis. Do not merge them because combining features feels safer. A combination must explain the causal role of every part.

## 7. Reality check: viable, feasible and honest

### Viability and adoption

- Who has a reason to sustain this after the prototype?

- Who pays, maintains, moderates, trains or takes responsibility?

- Does the intervention create a perverse incentive or transfer work to a less powerful person?
- What existing alternative might people prefer, and why?

## 9. Trust, safety, privacy and inclusion

### Trust and safety

- What must the person trust before taking the critical action?
- What harm can occur if the intervention is wrong, late, biased, misused or over-relied on?
- Who can see, change, sell, weaponise or misunderstand the information?
- What consent, explanation, undo, report, escalation or human handoff is needed?
- What should the system explicitly refuse to do?
- How will the prototype avoid implying a capability it does not have?

### Inclusion and accessibility

- Who cannot access the assumed device, language, literacy level, bandwidth, environment, time or confidence?
- Who is excluded by a visual, interaction, sensory, physical or cognitive assumption?
- What is the accessible equivalent of the critical action, not merely an accessibility afterthought?
- Does the intervention create stigma, pressure or risk in public / shared settings?
- What different cultural, family, workplace or community context changes the meaning of the interaction?

## 10. Incentives and second-order effects

- What behaviour does this reward, make easier or normalise?
- Who benefits if it works, and who loses power, time, income, privacy or agency?
- What could users game, outsource or avoid because of it?
- What happens after repeated use, not only after the happy-path demo?
- Does a metric or recommendation distort the behaviour it intends to help?
- What is the likely misuse, worst reasonable outcome and early warning sign?
- How would the team change course if that signal appears?

## 11. Design implications before design output

Do this before asking for UI, Figma or a deck. Better inputs make the first draft more specific; they do not replace human taste.

### Interaction and medium

- Is a screen necessary? If yes, why is this the right device and context?
- If no, what should be designed instead: service script, physical artefact, space, touchpoint, policy or ritual?
- What is the one critical interaction or proof moment a prototype must show?
- What information must be immediate, progressive, private, shared, ambient or tactile?
- What should be intentionally absent to keep the intervention clear?

### Visual communication job

- What must a user or judge understand in the first five seconds?
- What must become clearer after the core interaction?
- What should they feel: not a mood adjective, but a change in confidence, agency, urgency, safety or connection?
- What evidence, object, environment, language or behavioural detail can shape the visual language?
- What visual convention would falsely imply the wrong product category or make the concept feel generic?

## 17. Stop, proceed or test first

Use a verdict, not a numeric score.

### Stop or pause when

- the person, moment or cause remains generic after a focused pass
- the best version is still a familiar feature list with no distinct mechanism
- a non-app alternative makes the proposed medium unnecessary
- the core value cannot be shown honestly in the available time
- a trust, safety, accessibility or dependency risk has no credible answer
- the idea relies on evidence the team cannot verify
- the team cannot say why the design choices belong to this idea rather than an AI default

### Test first when

- one risky assumption could overturn the direction
- the mechanism is promising but the user response, context or dependency is unverified
- a small prototype, observation or conversation can choose between two plausible mechanisms

### Proceed with conditions when

- the causal chain is coherent, but named evidence, safety, accessibility, feasibility or authenticity conditions must be resolved before higher fidelity

### Proceed when

- the person, moment, evidence and mechanism form a believable chain
- a distinct core flow can be shown truthfully
- the leading risks have a proportionate mitigation or test
- the idea is understandable, retellable and materially different from the default response
