# FreTo reference: 2024 SUEDE overall winner

## Evidence boundary

- **Verified** means visible in the public [FreTo Devpost entry](https://devpost.com/software/freto-by-marlene-tania-kevin-aryan-caroline), its linked public [Canva deck](https://www.canva.com/design/DAGSFCqM4EU/v2j-xvl5a94I2w3oZ0nmcA/edit), or Aryan Rustagi’s public [case study](https://www.ary4n.com/freto).
- **Inference** is an interpretation of the material, not a claim about why judges chose FreTo.
- FreTo placed first in the 2024 event. That is evidence that its **whole package** worked in that context, not causal proof that every feature, framework, slide, visual treatment, or claim produced the win. The public 2024 criteria remain the governing historical comparison, not this precedent. See [JUDGING.md](JUDGING.md).

## ICP / persona / design problem

### Verified

- The primary persona is **Sarah Green, 21**, a busy university student with part-time work. Her goals include reducing food waste weekly, quick meal preparation and controlling grocery spend/avoiding take-out; stated pain points include no time to plan/cook, food waste costing money and uncertainty about leftovers. [Canva deck](https://www.canva.com/design/DAGSFCqM4EU/v2j-xvl5a94I2w3oZ0nmcA/edit)
- The case study describes Sarah as motivated more by saving money than sustainability, facing forgetfulness, inadequate tracking tools and time constraints. [Case study](https://www.ary4n.com/freto)
- The stated problem is that people do not adopt sustainable food practices because shopping and waste tracking are complex, time-constrained and lack accessible tools; monetary motivation dominates environmental motivation. The HMW asks for a digital way to simplify sustainable shopping and food tracking for busy individuals. [Case study](https://www.ary4n.com/freto)

### Inference

- The **effective ICP** is not “everyone who cares about sustainability.” It is a time-poor, budget-sensitive young adult who already buys groceries and discovers spoilage too late. That behavioural specificity makes QR/receipt capture and expiring-food meal prompts plausible.
- Reuse the logic, not Sarah: choose one real, evidence-backed person and a single failure moment where the intervention naturally belongs.

## Research, prioritisation and iteration proof

### Verified

- Before selecting the problem, the team considered four brief areas, allocated essential secondary research, and explicitly prioritised accessibility, lower cost, reduced resource/waste, reduced complexity, feasibility and innovation. [Case study](https://www.ary4n.com/freto)
- They used an **effort-versus-impact matrix** to compare the directions under the 24-hour constraint. Food-waste reduction was chosen as high impact/low effort after comparison; energy consumption was a close alternative. [Case study](https://www.ary4n.com/freto)
- Their stated research objectives were to understand food-waste behaviour/attitudes, barriers to reduction practices, current-solution effectiveness and incentives for sustainable habits. [Case study](https://www.ary4n.com/freto)
- The deck reports **40+ survey responses, seven interviews and online ethnography** under triangulation. The later case study instead says **seven survey responses**, so the public record conflicts on survey count. Cite the deck count when describing the presentation; do not merge them into a single certainty. [Canva deck](https://www.canva.com/design/DAGSFCqM4EU/v2j-xvl5a94I2w3oZ0nmcA/edit), [case study](https://www.ary4n.com/freto)
- They synthesised with affinity diagramming (deck: 68 notes, 22 groups, six themes), then produced HMWs, persona, journey map and behavioural patterns. [Canva deck](https://www.canva.com/design/DAGSFCqM4EU/v2j-xvl5a94I2w3oZ0nmcA/edit), [case study](https://www.ary4n.com/freto)
- Ideation used Crazy 8s; the top three ideas were selected by voting/discussion and potentially combined. [Case study](https://www.ary4n.com/freto)
- The case study says mid-fidelity testing included three think-alouds, two structured interviews, three SUS surveys and one unstructured interview. It reports two iteration cycles on the home page, with meal-plan task SUS improving from **39.0 to 83.5**. [Case study](https://www.ary4n.com/freto)
- Specific changes were attributed to feedback: reduce home-screen overload and establish hierarchy; clarify inventory headings and add a visible delete action; make savings more prominent than carbon alone; replace plant-growth indicators with clearer progress bars. [Case study](https://www.ary4n.com/freto)

### Inference

- Their strongest proof is not the count of frameworks. It is the visible chain **participant feedback → named interface change → measurable/observable improvement**. That is the proof pattern to reuse.
- An effort/impact matrix was useful because it made the 24-hour constraint explicit. It should not be treated as a universal selector: impact and effort estimates can be speculative, and the matrix alone cannot establish desirability or distinctiveness.

## Product flow and feature thesis

### Verified

- The declared product is a food-waste reduction app combining QR/receipt scanning, inventory management, AI-driven meal suggestions based on expiring ingredients, and personal sustainability/financial impact insights. [Devpost entry](https://devpost.com/software/freto-by-marlene-tania-kevin-aryan-caroline), [case study](https://www.ary4n.com/freto)
- The public deck’s main flow is **scan receipt or QR → update inventory → see/manage pantry → receive meal ideas → see impact**. [Canva deck](https://www.canva.com/design/DAGSFCqM4EU/v2j-xvl5a94I2w3oZ0nmcA/edit)
- The sustainability impact was reframed through user feedback as tangible monthly cost savings first, then water and carbon reduction. [Case study](https://www.ary4n.com/freto)
- The appendix frames a market distinction around more individual-item expiration prediction, barcode/receipt capture, supermarket integration, personalised metrics and educational content. These are presented as proposal/roadmap claims, not proof of a working integration. [Canva deck](https://www.canva.com/design/DAGSFCqM4EU/v2j-xvl5a94I2w3oZ0nmcA/edit)

### Inference

- The memorable mechanism is not “AI meal planning.” It is **removing the tracking burden at the grocery moment, then converting soon-to-expire inventory into a money-saving action**. The impact screen reinforces the motivation discovered in research.
- For a new response, make one critical loop demonstrable from trigger to better outcome. Do not imitate a multi-feature app stack just because FreTo had one.

## Visual language and pitch mechanics

### Verified visual observations

- The Canva deck uses a consistent playful editorial/scrapbook language: pale neutral backgrounds, repeating grid pattern, black display type, orange/green accents, torn-paper and sticky-note panels, hand-drawn arrows/scribbles, simple illustrations and oversized numbers. [Canva deck](https://www.canva.com/design/DAGSFCqM4EU/v2j-xvl5a94I2w3oZ0nmcA/edit)
- Its content modules recur: a strong slide title, one large number or central claim, then clustered notes/cards and arrows to guide scanning. Research artifacts are visually rendered rather than described as a verbal process list. [Canva deck](https://www.canva.com/design/DAGSFCqM4EU/v2j-xvl5a94I2w3oZ0nmcA/edit)
- The deck gives the product meaningful space only after research/synthesis, puts evidence of usability testing before the final flow, and moves longer competitor/empathy/user-modelling detail behind an appendix divider. [Canva deck](https://www.canva.com/design/DAGSFCqM4EU/v2j-xvl5a94I2w3oZ0nmcA/edit)

### Inference

- The presentation works as a **designed argument**: every major slide changes the audience’s confidence rather than simply documenting activity.
- The physical-note aesthetic supports an overnight, human-made process story, but it is stylistic, not a transferable reason for winning. A new deck needs its own visual rationale connected to its subject and user.

## What appears strong, what to reuse, what not to copy

### Strong in the public package

- A specific person, moment and motivation replaces generic sustainability rhetoric.
- Problem selection is evidenced, including a near-miss alternative and the time constraint.
- Multiple research methods, synthesis artifacts and a persona are made visible.
- Testing is not ceremonial: quotes and named changes connect findings to the final interface.
- The financial framing translates environmental value into a daily incentive.
- The flow is legible enough to explain without showing every possible feature.
- The appendix preserves depth without making the primary pitch a process dump.

### Reuse as structure

1. Start with a one-line promise, then show the precise human moment where it matters.
2. Use only enough evidence to establish scale and then immediately narrow to the user.
3. Show how research changed the problem statement, not just that research occurred.
4. Show real divergence and the criterion that selected the direction.
5. Demonstrate one end-to-end core loop.
6. Put test finding, iteration and outcome adjacent to the changed screens.
7. End the main story on consequence/value; keep defensive detail in an appendix.

### Do not copy

- Do not copy FreTo’s food-waste premise, Sarah persona, AI/barcode feature bundle, Canva scrapbook look, wording, screenshots, metrics or individual claims.
- Do not assume “AI” signals innovation. In the public materials, feasibility and data-integration claims are not independently demonstrated.
- Do not use research counts or SUS improvements as a decoration. Test the new concept with relevant people and state what actually changed.
- Do not turn the deck into 23 required slides. The sequence is a precedent, not a format mandate; timing and official 2026 submission rules may demand a smaller or different artifact.

## Gaps and weaknesses worth correcting

- **Evidence inconsistency:** the deck says 40+ surveys while the case study says seven. A future deck should maintain one evidence ledger and qualify samples, recruitment and limitations. [Canva deck](https://www.canva.com/design/DAGSFCqM4EU/v2j-xvl5a94I2w3oZ0nmcA/edit), [case study](https://www.ary4n.com/freto)
- **Persona breadth:** the case study acknowledges testing was restricted mainly to young adults/students and calls for varied age groups and household sizes. This makes broad “busy individuals” claims weaker than the student-focused evidence. [Case study](https://www.ary4n.com/freto)
- **Usability caveat:** the author also notes confusion around terminology and flows, information overload and a need for stronger typography/information hierarchy. The slide deck proves iteration, but not that every core flow became easy. [Case study](https://www.ary4n.com/freto)
- **Feasibility boundary:** proposed supermarket, API, barcode-database and ML connections are future-facing. A pitch should mark what is prototyped, simulated and external-dependency-dependent plainly. [Canva deck](https://www.canva.com/design/DAGSFCqM4EU/v2j-xvl5a94I2w3oZ0nmcA/edit)
- **Pitch economy:** the public deck is research-rich, with duplicated affinity pages and a long appendix. It has useful evidence, but a live presentation should prioritise the smallest proof set that makes the mechanism undeniable.
- **Competitive proof:** the appendix makes useful competitor assertions, but its source/method detail is limited in the deck. A new project should cite and time-stamp competitor checks, and avoid claims that a market has a “noticeable lack” without a reproducible basis.

## Proposed upgraded SUEDE deck template (exploratory, not a settled team decision)

This is an **inference-led template** for a short live pitch plus appendix. It preserves FreTo’s evidence chain while increasing truthfulness, speed and emphasis on the actual intervention. Apply the required SUEDE judge review before selecting it for the 2026 entry.

| Main slide | One job | Proof artifact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Retellable premise | State the person, moment, intervention and outcome in one sentence. | One authentic hero visual, not a feature collage. |
| 2. The moment | Dramatise the exact behavioural context and cost of inaction. | Observed quote, diary moment or concise scenario, clearly labelled. |
| 3. Why this is the problem | Establish scale/cause with only the evidence needed. | Two to three sourced facts; sources visible. |
| 4. Insight → design requirement | Show what research changed in the brief. | Theme/quote → requirement mapping. |
| 5. Why this direction | Show real mechanism-level divergence and selection. | Four paradigms, selection criteria and rejected assumptions. |
| 6. The core mechanism | Explain the non-obvious move in one diagram. | Trigger → action → response → user outcome. |
| 7. Live core flow | Demonstrate one end-to-end path, including system state. | High-fidelity prototype or honest simulation label. |
| 8. Failure, trust and inclusion | Answer what can go wrong and who could be excluded. | Error/recovery state, consent/data boundary and accessibility choice. |
| 9. Test → iteration → effect | Prove the prototype changed because of people, not preference. | Before/after screen with participant finding and result. |
| 10. Why it matters / close | Return to the person and make the outcome memorable. | One outcome metric/leading indicator and final one-line premise. |

**Appendix:** detailed methods/sample limits, raw synthesis, competitor comparison with sources, scope/feasibility, prototype truth table, additional flows, AI/third-party disclosures and credits. Keep the primary deck visual system specific to the 2026 concept rather than borrowing FreTo’s treatment.

## Judge-review checkpoint for adopting this template

This reference itself makes no settled product or pitch decision.
