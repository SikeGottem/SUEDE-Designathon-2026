## What the team has said about the pitch

- **Sell the product, not the struggle.**
  - Ethan initially expected the presentation to cover process problems and difficulties.
  - The team challenged that approach and converged on a product-led live pitch.
  - Process belongs only where research or testing changed a decision; the rest belongs in the appendix.
  - Source: [8d391 transcript](../WIKI/TRANSCRIPTS/2026-09-03-8d39172c-4cee-5766-97e7-90708888daf3.md).

- **Make it understandable without specialist knowledge.**
  - The team used Steve Jobs-style launches as a reference for explaining a technical idea simply.
  - The problem should make sense to a non-specialist or “grandma,” even if that person has never experienced it.
  - Source: [Recording 14](../WIKI/TRANSCRIPTS/2026-09-04-new-recording-14.md).

- **Use humour only when it explains.**
  - One or two moments of wit may help memorability.
  - Employer-specific jokes and forced comedy were considered risky.
  - The useful reference was a joke that also clarified how an interaction worked.
  - Source: [Recording 14](../WIKI/TRANSCRIPTS/2026-09-04-new-recording-14.md).

- **Start the presentation before the product is finished.**
  - The team explicitly said problem framing and research can be built into the deck while the solution continues.
  - Ethan volunteered to begin the presentation once the team had converged on friendship appreciation as the working problem.
  - Sources: [ffd4 transcript](../WIKI/TRANSCRIPTS/2026-09-04-ffd4b6fb-b877-5063-8311-7b886cbaf904.md) and [57f27 transcript](../WIKI/TRANSCRIPTS/2026-09-04-57f27d6d-511d-569c-ba4c-5b84cf5be137.md).

- **Open from the receiver’s experience.**
  - The team proposed asking when the audience last received a handwritten or appreciation letter and how it felt.
  - Ethan’s real care package and physical letter were proposed as the concrete emotional example.
  - The team repeatedly said to “sell the feeling” and the narrative before showing features.
  - Sources: [Recording 19](../WIKI/TRANSCRIPTS/2026-09-04-new-recording-19.md) and [356211 transcript](../WIKI/TRANSCRIPTS/2026-09-04-356211db-c212-5765-bcbb-205d615e351d.md).

- **Reveal the unwritten rule through a precise tension.**
  - Ordinary-day appreciation may be read as too intense, romantic, farewell-like, alarming or response-seeking.
  - Birthdays, farewells, crises and milestones appear to provide social permission that an ordinary Tuesday does not.
  - This is still a team hypothesis, not a population claim.
  - Sources: [532244 transcript](../WIKI/TRANSCRIPTS/2026-09-04-5322449d-86be-5681-abb6-09cbdf584c15.md) and [57f27 transcript](../WIKI/TRANSCRIPTS/2026-09-04-57f27d6d-511d-569c-ba4c-5b84cf5be137.md).

- **Make the channel question visible.**
  - The team specifically requested the same message shown as an ordinary SMS and as the proposed crafted object.
  - It also requested a comparison across communication formats, especially meaning, visible effort, delivery friction, ownership and ability to revisit.
  - The repeated “Goldilocks” hypothesis is that ordinary chat may feel too disposable while physical mail may create too much friction.
  - The deck must answer the judge’s obvious question: “Why not just send a physical letter?”
  - Source: [532244 transcript](../WIKI/TRANSCRIPTS/2026-09-04-5322449d-86be-5681-abb6-09cbdf584c15.md).

- **Show one multimedia experience, not a feature inventory.**
  - Ideas included personal writing, photographs, music, video, voice, drawings, stickers and custom composition.
  - The team moved from “digital letter” to “digital artefact” because a letter alone felt too familiar.
  - A receiver-owned archive or cabinet was repeatedly discussed as a way to keep and revisit past pieces.
  - A late capture adds a candidate reveal microinteraction where the recipient sees the sender's words appear as if they are being written. The backspace detail is too garbled to specify, and the interaction remains an exploratory product idea rather than a deck requirement.
  - Sources: [084643 transcript](../WIKI/TRANSCRIPTS/2026-09-04-0846430d-0a6c-5674-a89d-0378f1484d38.md), [Recording 17](../WIKI/TRANSCRIPTS/2026-09-04-new-recording-17.md), [57f27 transcript](../WIKI/TRANSCRIPTS/2026-09-04-57f27d6d-511d-569c-ba4c-5b84cf5be137.md) and [b7cab transcript](../WIKI/TRANSCRIPTS/2026-09-04-b7cab12f-1dd1-5f97-aa58-68fb22527e35.md).

- **The intended climax is an audience-facing reveal.**
  - The proposed final beat is: “we wrote something for you too,” followed by a QR code and a working reveal experience.
  - Envelope-opening motion, a letter sliding upward and other object metaphors were explored.
  - This can demonstrate the flow, but a scan, smile or emotional reaction is not evidence that the product changes a norm.
  - Sources: [Recording 20](../WIKI/TRANSCRIPTS/2026-09-04-new-recording-20.md), [Recording 19](../WIKI/TRANSCRIPTS/2026-09-04-new-recording-19.md) and [356211 transcript](../WIKI/TRANSCRIPTS/2026-09-04-356211db-c212-5765-bcbb-205d615e351d.md).

- **Anticipate Q&A without overstuffing the pitch.**
  - The team wants obvious objections answered before Q&A, especially medium necessity, adoption, sincerity, reply pressure and why existing formats are insufficient.
  - A pitch cannot eliminate all questions. Defensive evidence should live in a structured appendix rather than expanding every main slide.

## Exact requested content

1. Front page with team name, team number and member names.
2. Opening question about the last meaningful letter or appreciation someone received.
3. Ethan’s care-package and physical-letter story, subject to consent and access to the real object.
4. The same words shown in ordinary messaging and in the designed experience.
5. A comparison of communication formats.
6. A direct answer to “Why not a physical letter?”
7. Product states that demonstrate multimedia, authorship, ownership and revisitation.
8. A clear opening or reveal sequence rather than decorative animation.
9. A final generic, opt-in audience artefact reached through QR and a no-device alternative.
10. Research, personas and problem framing in the presentation, with fuller process evidence in the appendix or Figma board.
11. AI and third-party-use disclosure.
12. Accurate, appealing Devpost images.

The transcripts do not request a market-size slide, business model, roadmap, technical-architecture diagram, feature grid or competitor quadrant. None should be added by default.

## Changes of mind resolved for the deck

- **Process diary versus product pitch:** product pitch wins; show only process that changed the design.
- **App versus web/link:** unresolved; a mobile website was discussed for QR practicality, but the medium has not passed comparison.
- **Letter versus artefact:** the team prefers broader multimedia territory, but no product mechanism is settled.
- **Broad appreciation versus a male-specific audience:** no gender-specific claim is supported; recruit and test broadly.
- **Physical is emotionally strongest versus digital can equal it:** this is the central test, not a fact to announce.
- **Generic room demo versus judge-personalised demo:** judge research and personalisation were rejected as invasive. Keep any demo generic and opt-in.
- **Humour for memorability versus humour for explanation:** only explanatory humour remains.
- **Animation versus clarity:** motion remains only when it explains sequence, state or causality.

## Evidence and ethics boundary

- Raw transcript enthusiasm is not user validation.
- Team anecdotes must be labelled as anecdotes and used with consent.
- No generated persona, fake quote, fabricated response, purchased response or “botted” research can enter the deck, appendix or Q&A.
- Do not claim that all friends, men or young people share the rule.
- Do not call an audience reaction validation.
- Keep chat, voice, physical letters and non-digital rituals as genuine controls.
- Any audience interaction must be optional, private, accessible, reply-free and available without QR or a personal device.

## Strongest transcript-led narrative

```text
Receive something meaningful
→ notice why ordinary-day appreciation feels socially unusual
→ compare what the same words mean in different containers
→ derive the requirements for sincere, low-pressure care
→ reveal one chosen mechanism
→ watch the sender-to-receiver outcome happen
→ show what real testing changed
→ return to the audience as the receiver
```

The strongest current visual spine is the emotional opening, the same-message comparison and the audience callback. The weakest link is the unproven jump from “physical letters feel meaningful” to “this particular digital experience changes the unwritten rule.”
