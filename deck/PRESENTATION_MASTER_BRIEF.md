## 2. Transcript provenance and uncertainty

The current corpus contains 27 captures in `WIKI/TRANSCRIPTS/`, including recordings 14–20 and Chronicle/Discord captures. The raw files are the durable record. Several are machine transcripts, long recordings include ambient noise and repeated fragments, and many speakers are unattributed. A transcript can establish that the team discussed an idea; it cannot establish who owns it, that the team selected it, that users want it, or that a claim is true.

Use a source pointer whenever a transcript-specific point survives into a deck or appendix. Do not invent a clean quote from garbled audio. Team anecdotes require consent, clear attribution and a distinction from research.

| Provenance class | May support | Must not support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Settled current instruction | Production constraint or stated team direction | General user truth |
| Clear team proposal | Candidate concept, visual reference, demo beat or question | A final feature or promise |
| Team anecdote | A consented illustrative story | Population claim or test result |
| Machine-transcribed / unclear | Retrieval lead and explicitly labelled uncertainty | Exact quote, speaker attribution or factual precedent |
| Published source | Bounded factual claim with citation | A claim beyond its population/context |
| Primary test | Observed response and changed design | Broad market proof |

## 3. Pitch thesis and non-negotiable guardrails

### Provisional thesis

> For friends who already care but do not share a comfortable script for direct appreciation, an ordinary sincere message can become socially ambiguous. The opportunity is not “make people grateful”; it is to test whether a deliberately made, receiver-controlled gesture can let an existing thought arrive without becoming a conversation debt.

This is a research frame, not a final product claim. The team must not claim that all friends, men, young people or digital messages share this condition.

### The intended narrative promise

The audience should leave able to repeat: **“They found an unwritten rule around ordinary-day appreciation, tested what makes it awkward or meaningful, and designed one believable way to change the receiving contract.”**

### Guardrails

- Do not pitch a generic gratitude, mental-health, relationship-maintenance or AI-writing app.
- Do not assume a digital product is better than a physical letter, voice note, practical help, ordinary message or doing nothing. The medium must earn itself.
- Do not frame men as emotionally deficient. Gender, sport and peer-culture material are recruitment and research questions unless evidence establishes a bounded pattern.
- Do not present a daily reminder, streak, random assignment or automated prompt as care without testing provenance and obligation effects.
- Do not claim that the final audience QR scan, smile or applause validates the concept.
- Do not personalise a judge demo from private research. The audience artefact must be generic, opt-in, reply-free and available without a personal device.
- Do not make the product’s emotional writing AI-authored. AI can support layout, assets, technical execution and controlled exploration; humans own intimate content, selection, testing, final edits and disclosure.
- Do not use a physical-object metaphor as decoration. Any envelope, bottle, bird or cabinet must have an interaction meaning or be removed.

### Recommended time budget

Opening + rule + evidence: ~130 seconds. Requirements + medium comparison: ~50 seconds. Reveal + mechanism + sender/receiver flow: ~150 seconds. Trust + test + close: ~70 seconds. This leaves a small transition buffer. If content grows, remove an unproven feature before shortening the receiver path.

### Research/test story to earn in the deck

1. State the initial problem hypothesis and what could falsify it.
2. Recruit both likely and negative cases: direct expressers, sentimental-artefact skeptics, high-contact friends, low-contact friends, low-capacity recipients and people who prefer practical care.
3. Test matched message containers with identical content: ordinary chat, physical/crafted control and one bounded digital concept. Measure interpretation, pressure, personalness, safety, willingness to send/receive and what they would do next.
4. Show only one actual finding that changed one actual interaction or scope decision.
5. Keep sample, recruitment, limitations and all raw questions in the appendix. Never use a research count as decoration.

## 7. Provisional ICP and persona policy

The working ICP is a **behavioural situation**, not a demographic label:

> A person with a close or still-valued low-contact friend, a specific appreciative thought, and a reason to hesitate because ordinary chat feels too casual, too intense, or too conversation-opening; physical mail or a large gift feels disproportionate or impractical.

They are not necessarily a daily user. The design may be more credible as an occasional ritual than an engagement product. The primary person initiating the flow is the **sender**; the recipient’s interpretation is equally important to success. The key job is not “remember to care,” but “turn a thought I already have into a gesture that will be understood without demanding anything back.”

**ICP falsifiers:** If people readily use existing chat/voice/physical options; if recipients find a no-reply artefact eerie; if the main barrier is simply wording; if spontaneous gestures are unwelcome; or if only a narrow occasion context works, narrow or abandon the concept.

## 8. Demo runbook and prototype truth boundaries

### Demo purpose

The demo must make a judge understand the rule, the user’s decision, the receiver’s changed conditions and the boundary of what works. It must not act out emotional impact as if that were evidence.

### Proposed generic demo path

1. **Trigger:** sender remembers a concrete thing about a friend.
2. **Authorship:** sender chooses one mode and writes/records their own content. Optional prompts may help recall, but must not generate the intimacy.
3. **Framing:** sender sees exactly what the recipient will receive and whether reply is optional/closed.
4. **Arrival:** recipient receives a bounded invitation, with no urgency implication.
5. **Opening:** recipient chooses when to open; any reveal shows sequence, not decoration.
6. **Completion:** recipient can keep, close, decline or respond, depending on the selected mechanism. The design must never falsely promise “no pressure.”
7. **Callback:** optional generic QR lets the room experience a non-personal example; no-device route is equivalent.

### Truth table

| Area | Allowed claim during prototype | Must label / cannot claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sender composition | “This clickable flow demonstrates authored text/media selection.” | No claim that AI understands the relationship or writes the content |
| Delivery | “The prototype simulates delivery/arrival.” | Do not imply push delivery, identities or timing work unless built |
| Receiver opening | “This demonstrates the intended interaction.” | Do not imply it reduces pressure without testing |
| Cabinet/archive | “Concept state” only until chosen and built | Do not imply long-term keeping behaviour |
| QR room demo | “Optional demo access” | Not a live experiment or proof of adoption |
| Motion | “Demonstrates state sequence” | Never claim Figma/HTML animation is a production implementation |
| Privacy / deletion | Show only real controls | Do not narrate policies absent from prototype |

### Failure and recovery states that must exist before a final demo

- Wrong recipient or changed mind before delivery.
- Unexpected/undesired arrival: decline, close or delete without explaining.
- Recipient cannot or does not want to use audio/motion/media.
- No device / QR cannot scan / network fails: static equivalent and speaker fallback.
- Content is private: no public feed, default sharing or surprise audience personalisation.
- Message is read as crisis/romance/farewell: framing/intent cannot guarantee interpretation, so test what can reduce ambiguity and disclose remaining uncertainty.

## 11. AI/human authorship, brand and production workflow

### Authorship policy

Humans frame the problem, choose the audience and interaction, decide what is ethically acceptable, author/rewrite intimate content, select references, draw the signature assets, test with people and approve final output. AI may help produce divergent HTML concepts, structure content, code the prototype, create disposable layout explorations and surface counterarguments. It cannot create evidence, decide the final taste, generate user quotes, supply the emotional message, or stand in for research.

For submission, record material tools, prompts/assets that survive into final work, external sources, Cecelia’s original icon/branding contribution, test-driven changes and which prototype states are simulated.

### Cecelia’s future hand-drawn visual scope

Once the mechanism is selected, hand drawing should cover only a coherent, reusable system: product mark; a constrained icon set for core actions/states; selected delivery/object motifs; a small set of texture/line primitives; and any custom lettering chosen as a real signature. It should not cover every utility icon or turn the UI into decorative sketching. The product state must still read without the illustrations.

### Tool workflow

1. **Research + content:** Wiki and this brief retain claim state, sources, decisions and test changes.
2. **Exploration:** use AI to make several bounded HTML concepts. Each labels assumptions, source anchors, what is simulated and one question it is meant to answer. Do not call an HTML concept the source of truth.
3. **Human selection:** team compares concepts against evidence, test needs, trust and authorship. One visual lead makes the material redirection.
4. **Prototype:** keep the chosen core flow functional in HTML/web when speed is valuable. Use a mobile link/QR only if it supports the selected behaviour.
5. **Figma:** after a concept is chosen, recreate/compose the editable selected states using proper auto layout and components. Figma is for editable design source and review, not a blind HTML import. Verify direct plugin capabilities rather than claiming a one-click conversion.
6. **Slides:** use Figma Slides as editable visual source after a small verified slide spike. HTML/Remotion can supply bounded motion/reference assets, not a second independently edited deck. Canva/FreTo is structural precedent only.
7. **Final:** validate deck content, links, timing, notes, source labels, appendix, offline/static demo, AI disclosure and document history.

## 14. Review checkpoint
