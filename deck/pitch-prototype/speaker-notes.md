## Live demo run sheet

- **2:55–3:05 — Name:** name the person; this makes an ordinary thought explicitly personal.
- **3:05–3:20 — Make:** show authorship and explain why visible effort matters without exploring every tool.
- **3:20–3:35 — Seal:** show the deliberate transition out of editing and explain why the finite ending creates closure.
- **3:35–3:55 — Send:** generate the receiver link and connect that choice to lower receiving friction.
- **3:55–4:15 — Open:** use the prepared receiver device or window to open that link without an account, on their own terms.
- **4:15–4:25 — Keep/remove:** show keep/reopen and the close/remove options, connecting them to receiver control without implying permanence.
- **4:25–4:35 — Finish:** land the receiver-controlled ending: **“The message can be complete when it arrives.”** Use pre-opened live tabs as the recovery path; do not add another deck slide or replace the demo with a video.

## Live handling

- If time slips, shorten the map narration and creation step. Do not cut What / Why / How or the receiver-controlled ending.
- If asked about any blank: the team refused to invent the number. It will be filled only if the completed study supports it cleanly.
- If asked “why not text, voice or a call?”: those remain valid controls. The matched-format test must determine whether an authored object changes impact, effort or comfort.
- If asked “who is this for?”: name the need-state first: people caught between an ordinary text and a disproportionate physical gesture. University students, long-distance connections, close friends and close family are included examples, not validated or exclusive segments.
- If asked “why an app?”: the digital form is being tested for the ease of messaging plus deliberate authorship and asynchronous receiving; superiority is not assumed.
