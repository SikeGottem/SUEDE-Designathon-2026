# Warm & Fuzzies presentation review and proposed changes

Date: 2026-09-05  
Status: proposal for team review; the broader rebuild has not been applied.

## Executive verdict

The deck is visually strong enough for rehearsal, but it is not ready for judged delivery.

The biggest problem is not styling. Slides 5, 6 and 8 use invented rehearsal percentages, decimal ratings, chart positions and circle sizes with the visual authority of completed research. The small disclaimers do not neutralise a title such as `Survey results` or the precision of `82%`, `71%` and nine 1–5 scores.

The strongest product idea is also not yet the main story. The deck currently sells a **Goldilocks communication medium**: more meaningful than a text and easier than a letter. That is understandable but familiar. The more distinctive idea is a **different receiving contract**: one finite, self-authored object that the receiver can open, keep or remove without a required reply or sender read state.

Recommendation: use Goldilocks to explain the design constraints, then make the receiver-controlled ending the product thesis.

## What is already working

- A memorable, team-authored paper-and-ink identity.
- A compact 10-slide live spine with a 4:35 target and 1:25 buffer.
- A good ordinary-day problem territory.
- Slide 4's occasion-versus-ordinary-day contrast.
- A clear full maker-to-receiver demo rather than a static feature tour.
- A real receiver ending: defer, open, keep or remove, with no required reply.
- An optional QR, readable URL and no-phone route.

## Five critical faults

1. **P0 — evidence looks fabricated.** Practice values currently resemble completed participant results.
2. **P0 — conclusions outrun the evidence.** The deck jumps from possible awkwardness to a gift-impact/digital-friction solution requirement.
3. **P0 — the product proposition is generic.** `Thoughtfulness of a letter + ease of a text` can be retold as a digital card.
4. **P1 — there is no validated first user or moment.** Uni students, long-distance connections, friends and family are examples, not one target.
5. **P1 — process is hidden.** Judges see conclusions but not which observation or test caused each design decision.

## Proposed 10-slide narrative

Process should be the causal story, not a separate double-diamond slide.

### 1. Project title and identity

- **Focal point:** `Rethinking when we show appreciation.`
- **Then:** firefly and Warm & Fuzzies lockup.
- **Quiet layer:** verified team number and member names when supplied.
- **Already applied:** yes.

### 2. Human hook

- Keep the sender-framed question.
- Spoken boundary: not a birthday or major event; just because.
- Visually emphasise only `appreciation or gratitude?` during the pause.

### 3. Starting hypothesis

- Add a small honest label: `where we started`.
- Main line: `We appreciate our friends and family. We just do not always show it.`
- Spoken process: this was the team's assumption, not a result.
- Visual emphasis lands on `show it.`

### 4. Reframing the problem

- Proposed title: `We looked for what makes an ordinary day different.`
- Reveal birthday, farewell and milestone as possible ready-made scripts.
- Then fade those examples and reveal `ordinary day — awkward / too much / intense`.
- Final process transition: `We shifted from asking whether people care to asking when care feels socially permitted.`
- Keep the idea visibly labelled as a working hypothesis.

### 5. What secondary research made us test

- Proposed title: `Psychology gave us three mechanisms to test.`
- One active channel per click; previous channels fade.
- Quick text: tone can be misread.
- Voice/video: voice can feel closer.
- Letter/gift: visible effort can signal thoughtfulness.
- Final build: Goldilocks becomes a **design question**, not a discovered answer.
- Spoken boundary: published work made the mechanisms plausible; it did not prove they matter in this context.

### 6. What primary research changed

- Until real data exists, title it `We designed the research around three questions.`
- **What:** does a specific appreciative thought go unexpressed?
- **Why:** what prediction or social interpretation stops it?
- **How:** what do people do instead, and which tested change alters interpretation?
- When real results arrive, retitle it `What our research changed` and reveal one genuine result at a time.
- Do not use the current percentages, decimal scores or grouped-media equivalence in judged delivery.

### 7. Who we test first

- Proposed title: `That left one specific need-state.`
- Use the rings as context, not market segmentation.
- Interim centre copy: `A close but distance-separated relationship, one specific appreciative thought, and no format that fits the moment.`
- Treat university students, long-distance family and close friends as recruitment examples only.
- Replace the examples with one evidenced first context after interviews.

### 8. The missing process pivot

- Replace the repeated If / And / Then percentages.
- First build: `We stopped designing a better message.`
- Second build: `We designed a different receiving contract.`
- Then show three finding-to-decision links:
  - Meaning must be visible → self-authored object.
  - The gesture needs an edge → finite and sealable.
  - Receiving may create reply debt → receiver controls open, keep or remove; no reply UI or read receipt.
- Keep a small boundary: these are hypotheses embodied in the prototype, not validated outcomes.

### 9. Solution

- Recommended line: `Warm & Fuzzies turns one genuine thought into a finite digital keepsake that the receiver opens and ends on their own terms.`
- Avoid `private` until the forwardable bearer-link model earns that claim.
- Make `finite digital keepsake` and `on their own terms` the two visual anchors.
- Switch immediately to the product.

### 10. Causal demo and close

- Keep QR participation optional.
- Demonstrate `name → make → seal → send → open → keep/remove`.
- Explain why each step exists instead of listing features.
- Final spoken callback while staying on slide 10:
  - `We began with a gap between a text and a letter.`
  - `What we ended up redesigning was the social contract around receiving care.`
  - `The message can be complete when it arrives.`

## Proposed visual hierarchy system

Every click gets one visual boss.

| Layer | Treatment | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current spoken point | Navy or brighter blue, 64–110px | The one thing the audience should see now. |
| Supporting mechanism | Steel or pale blue, 26–42px | The proof or context that earns the point. |
| Method/source/navigation | Muted navy, 14–18px | Available for scrutiny without competing. |
| Design decision | Cream field with navy text/border | One earned conclusion, not decoration. |

Build behaviour:

- New material enters at full contrast.
- Previous material remains as context at roughly 35–45% contrast.
- Slides must not accumulate every item at equal weight.
- Use bright blue only for the clause currently being explained.
- Use cream only for the HMW or the slide-8 process pivot.
- Keep the firefly steel in ordinary contexts and navy when it represents the product or target.
- Hide `next`, fullscreen and arrow controls during projection.

## Priority order

### Before any judged pitch

1. Replace or remove every rehearsal percentage, rating, coordinate and circle-size claim.
2. Run a matched sender/receiver comparison across chat, voice, Warm & Fuzzies and one non-digital control.
3. Select one real first user/context and one negative case.
4. Rewrite slides 3–9 around what the research actually changed.
5. Add verified team number and member names to slide 1.
6. Remove or qualify `private`.

### After evidence is real

1. Apply the one-visual-boss hierarchy to each build.
2. Replace slide 8 with the receiving-contract pivot.
3. Add a judge-facing appendix for methods, raw evidence, iterations, prototype truth, references and AI/third-party disclosure.
4. Test every final state at projector distance.
5. Rehearse the QR and live flow on the event network and two physical devices.

## Decision required from the team

Choose the primary thesis before the next broad rebuild:

- **A — Goldilocks medium:** a better balance between text and letter. Easier to explain, but more generic and dependent on comparative ratings.
- **B — Receiver-controlled social contract — recommended:** a finite authored object whose receiver controls the ending. More distinctive, more aligned to the unwritten-rule brief, and more honestly demonstrated by the current prototype.

The proposal assumes option B. Goldilocks remains the constraint-setting research question, not the final innovation claim.
