# Problem presentation prototype

## Status and scope

- Exploratory two-slide HTML visual spike for `lived-moment` and `unwritten-rule`.
- `deck/deck-spec.json` remains the content source of truth.
- This is not a selected final deck direction and does not validate the product.
- The real-object slot stays visibly unresolved until its owner consents and supplies the asset.
- Visual lead for this spike: `frontend-design`, constrained by the current prototype and the SUEDE authenticity gate.

## Evidence boundary

The safe problem statement is narrow: in gratitude-letter studies, senders overestimated recipient awkwardness and underestimated positive response. The team's ordinary-day, occasion-permission, and reply-pressure framing remains a working hypothesis. There is no completed project test yet.

Do not turn this spike into any of these claims:

- people do not appreciate their friends;
- all friends, all men, or any demographic share this response;
- ordinary chat is meaningless or physical letters are inherently authentic;
- the prototype reduces reply pressure;
- digital is better than chat, voice, a physical note, or a non-digital ritual.

## Compact visual contract

- **Communication job:** make the audience feel that existing care can become harder to express when an accepted occasion is removed, without presenting that interpretation as universal.
- **Source anchors:** the birthday-versus-ordinary-Tuesday question; one deliberate finite object; the prototype's warm paper, black ink, single muted object colour, sparse space, thin rules, and irregular handwriting; the unchanged sentence `I appreciate you.`
- **Reference delta:** borrow the prototype's object grammar, scale, palette, and restraint. Do not borrow its phone frame, app controls, envelope icon, product states, or unvalidated solution language. Borrow sparse ink and book-cover colour from the references, not the `grug` brand, mascot, copy, or compositions.
- **Rejected defaults:** no app screenshot enlarged into a slide; no card grid or coloured side rail; no cream scrapbook collage, tape, stickers, or decorative doodles; no gradient, glow, pill, fake quote, persona, or metric.
- **Signature move:** the note and its words remain physically unchanged while the surrounding context shifts from `a birthday` to `an ordinary Tuesday`. The visual change makes the hypothesised social permission visible.
- **Restraint:** no celebratory iconography and no decorative envelope. The one unresolved real object and the one context-changing note carry both slides.
- **Human decision:** Ethan explicitly asked for the problem section to use the same visual style as the working product prototype. Composition remains exploratory until he selects or redirects it after seeing the render.

First notice: a finite made object, then the unchanged sentence. This best carries the product prototype's visual logic into a problem story. Main risk: the object can imply a selected solution if the placeholder or caveat is removed.

### B. Sentence first

First notice: the language itself. This is clearest at thumbnail size, but the diptych repeats the sentence and can flatten the tension into a familiar before/after layout.

### C. Silence first

```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                              │
│                                              │
│  a thought you had, but did not send         │
```

First notice: withheld expression and empty space. This is emotionally sharper, but current evidence does not establish unsent-message prevalence, so it risks making the visual more certain than the research.

## Pitch preflight

**Stage:** pitch visual spike. This verdict permits only the two scoped problem slides as an exploratory renderer.

| Judging lens | Effect | Current basis | Condition or risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brief fit | strengthens | The unwritten rule is made perceptible through a context change. | Keep the rule labelled as a working hypothesis. |
| Problem identification | strengthens conditionally | The moment is specific: a close friend, a sincere ordinary-day thought, and uncertainty about interpretation. | No target demographic, frequency, or local effect is established. |
| Solution approach | neutral | No solution claim appears in these slides. | The object must not imply that a digital keepsake has been selected or validated. |
| Design innovation | strengthens | The unchanged note makes social permission visible without a feature explanation. | A stationery aesthetic alone would be familiar and insufficient. |
| Visual communication | strengthens | One object, one sentence, and one state change create a legible hierarchy. | The placeholder needs a real consented asset before final use. |
| Presentation skills | strengthens | The opening question and context shift can be retold after one viewing. | Rehearsal must confirm the internal beat lands within 55 seconds. |
| Evidence quality | weakens | External evidence supports only a narrower sender prediction gap and there are no completed project tests. | Keep limitations on-slide and in the existing speaker notes. |

| Challenge lens | Strongest challenge | Current answer and blocking unknown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assumption and inversion | Appreciation may already be expressed through action, humour, or presence. | The copy says neither that care is absent nor that direct words are always better. Which people want another channel remains unknown. |
| Behaviour and context | `Friends` and `ordinary day` can still be broad. | The spike uses one close-friend, ordinary-Tuesday moment. Recurrence and consequence need direct evidence. |
| Medium necessity | The visual object could quietly preselect digital. | It is an honest asset slot, not product UI. Digital still has to beat or complement existing controls. |
| Alternatives | Chat, voice, paper, and ritual may already work better. | None is criticised here. The later matched-medium test remains required. |
| Human specificity | The team anecdote may not generalise. | No anecdote is fabricated. A real object appears only after consent. |
| Inclusion and accessibility | Irregular type can become difficult to read. | Large display sizes, high contrast, semantic HTML, keyboard control, and reduced motion are required. |
| Trust, safety and privacy | An intimate object can expose its owner or recipient. | No personal content or image is included. Consent remains a hard replacement gate. |
| Failure and recovery | The message may be read as crisis, romance, farewell, or obligation. | Those appear only as possible questions, not claimed reactions. The solution's recovery model is outside this spike. |
| Feasibility and demo truth | Motion can make a hypothesis feel proven. | The context transition is explanatory theatre only and is labelled accordingly. |
| Incentives and second-order effects | Framing direct expression as superior could shame other friendship styles. | The spike focuses on people who already have a thought they want to express and does not rank care languages. |
| Simplicity and retellability | Caveats could bury the human moment. | The visual story stays simple; the boundary is one concise footer plus existing speaker notes. |
| Authorship and distinctiveness | Warm paper and handwriting can become generic designathon styling. | The signature is structural: the unchanged note changes meaning only when the occasion changes. Ethan must still select or materially redirect the render. |

**Verdict:** Proceed with conditions.

- Keep the output labelled exploratory.
- Build only the two problem slides.
- Keep the hypothesis and evidence boundary visible.
- Preserve the unresolved consented-object slot.
- Do not let the visual imply a selected product or proven mechanism.
- Run the post-build authenticity and judge review on the real render.

**Weakest judging angle:** evidence quality, because the project has no completed sender/receiver test.

**Next evidence:** compare the same self-authored appreciation across ordinary chat, a physical or crafted control, and the rough digital object with senders and receivers separately.
