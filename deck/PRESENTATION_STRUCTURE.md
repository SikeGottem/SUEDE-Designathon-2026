# Presentation content and structure

## Current status

- **Working format:** 13 main slides for a six-minute heat pitch, plus a defensive appendix.
- **Constraint status:** the six-minute timing and 15-slide cap were captured during the opening session but still need confirmation against organiser slides or Slack.
- **Visual status:** intentionally brand-free. Use black text, white backgrounds, one ordinary sans-serif typeface and simple rules only.
- **Product status:** friendship appreciation is the chosen investigation. The exact audience, cause, medium, mechanism and product remain open.
- **Build boundary:** the problem and evidence half can begin now. Product, demo, iteration and closing claims remain visibly blocked until the relevant decisions and tests exist.

## The structure in one line

```text
Human receipt → unwritten rule → evidence → design requirements → real alternatives → one mechanism → complete demo → test-led change → audience callback
```

This is a confidence ladder, not a project diary. Every slide should change what the judges understand or believe.

## Working story question

> Why can “I appreciate you” feel natural on a birthday, but sound like goodbye on an ordinary Tuesday?

This is memorable pitch language, not established evidence. Keep it phrased as a question until local research confirms the occasion-permission frame.

## Main deck — 13 slides / 6:00

| # | ID | Time | Slide job and working content | Required proof | Status |
| ---: | --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `cover-premise` | 0:20 | **Orient the room.** Team name, team number, member names and one plain sentence containing the person, moment, rule, mechanism and intended outcome. Until the mechanism is selected, use a visibly incomplete content slot rather than a polished slogan. | Confirmed team details and selected one-line concept. | Blocked on name, number and mechanism. |
| 2 | `lived-moment` | 0:30 | **Start with receipt.** Ask when the audience last received something made deliberately to show appreciation. Use one real team-owned care-package or letter moment if the owner consents. | Real object, photograph or attributable anecdote; no invented persona or quote. | Structure ready; asset and consent pending. |
| 3 | `unwritten-rule` | 0:25 | **Make the invisible rule legible.** Explore why the same sincere words can be expected on a birthday but feel unusually intense on an ordinary day. Name the possible social interpretations without claiming they are universal. | Local critical incident or sender/receiver evidence; current external research only supports narrower hypotheses. | Draft question; final claim blocked. |
| 4 | `evidence-gap` | 0:30 | **Show what is known.** People expressing gratitude can overpredict recipient awkwardness; gratitude can also signal relational distance or create sincerity questions when prompted. The result is a tension, not a universal conclusion. | Two or three directly sourced findings plus visible limitations; add team-owned evidence when available. | Draft from sourced research. |
| 5 | `evidence-to-requirements` | 0:25 | **Show what evidence changes.** The experience must preserve sender authorship, protect receiver agency, clarify what the gesture means and allow the moment to end without compulsory reciprocity. | Finding → design requirement mapping. | Draft; refine after interviews. |
| 6 | `same-words-test` | 0:25 | **Make the medium earn its place.** Keep the words identical while comparing ordinary chat, a physical or crafted control and a rough digital artefact. Ask what changes in sincerity, awkwardness, ownership, effort, alarm and reply pressure. | Matched stimuli and separate sender/receiver observations. Do not present a speculative “Goldilocks” chart as data. | Structure ready; result blocked on test. |
| 7 | `concept-reveal` | 0:20 | **Reveal only the selected response.** Product name and one sentence explaining the distinct mechanism. It must be more than a prettier digital letter or a list of media types. | Selected audience, causal frame, medium and mechanism after review. | Blocked. |
| 8 | `mechanism-map` | 0:35 | **Explain the non-obvious move.** Trigger → deliberate creation → bounded delivery → receiver-controlled opening → safe completion or keeping. Show exactly where the unwritten rule changes. | Complete state model and honest prototype truth labels. | Blocked. |
| 9 | `sender-demo` | 0:35 | **Demo the sender moment.** Show why the sender acts now, what they author themselves and how the system avoids manufacturing intimacy. | Working or clearly simulated sender path. | Blocked. |
| 10 | `receiver-demo` | 0:35 | **Demo the payoff.** Show receipt, interpretation, opening, ownership and an explicit path where no reply is owed. Concentrate the richest visual/motion work here only if it explains the experience. | Working or clearly simulated receiver path; receiver testing. | Blocked. |
| 11 | `trust-and-recovery` | 0:30 | **Prove the idea survives real life.** Show unwanted contact, wrong recipient, delete/decline, privacy, reduced motion and a no-QR/no-device route. | Actual states and declared data/consent boundary. | Blocked. |
| 12 | `test-to-change` | 0:25 | **Show judgment, not ceremony.** Place participant finding → visible design change → observed effect together. State what the prototype proves and what remains simulated. | Completed test, before/after artefact and limitations. | Blocked on testing. |
| 13 | `callback-close` | 0:25 | **Return to the person and the rule.** State the changed social contract in one sentence. If used, offer the room a generic, optional artefact through QR and an equally visible no-device path. The reveal demonstrates the flow; it is not impact evidence. | Final working experience, access fallback and tested close. | Blocked. |

**Total:** 360 seconds.

For a seven-minute final, retain the same 13 slides and spend the additional minute on the live demo and test result. Do not add a feature slide merely because more time is available.

## What can be authored now

- Slide 2’s framing and asset slot.
- Slide 3 as a research question rather than a factual headline.
- Slide 4’s external evidence and visible limitations.
- Slide 5’s provisional evidence-to-requirement mapping.
- Slide 6’s comparison method and empty result areas.
- Stable slide IDs, timings, source labels, speaker-note prompts and appendix slots.

## What must stay empty or marked blocked

- Final product name and one-line promise.
- A single target persona or demographic claim.
- Claims about prevalence, masculinity, loneliness or harm.
- A claim that digital is more meaningful than chat or paper.
- The selected mechanism and medium.
- Product screens, feature claims and implementation detail.
- Test findings, quotes, numbers or before/after effects that do not yet exist.
- The final QR or demo link.

## Appendix order

1. **Exact brief and deliverables mapping** — every requirement and where it is answered.
2. **Research methods and sample boundary** — recruitment, protocol, sample size and limitations.
3. **Evidence ledger** — direct observations, external findings, inference and unknowns kept separate.
4. **Existing responses** — ordinary messaging, voice, physical letters, Slowly, Kudoboard, Letterloop and non-digital rituals.
5. **Full divergence** — baseline, embedded intervention, non-app response and inversion; why each was retained or rejected.
6. **Extended states** — privacy, consent, unwanted contact, errors, recovery, accessibility and reduced motion.
7. **Prototype truth table** — working, simulated, proposed and externally dependent behaviour.
8. **Testing** — scripts, matched stimuli, complete results and what changed.
9. **Feasibility and scope** — only dependencies needed to believe the core loop.
10. **AI, third-party material, document history, credits and sources.**
11. **Q&A attack sheet and backup demo access.**

## Brand-free construction rules

- White background, black text and neutral grey only.
- One ordinary sans-serif typeface in regular and bold weights.
- Left-aligned claim headlines; one dominant composition per slide.
- No colour palette, gradients, glows, shadows, textures, rounded-card system, illustrations or decorative icons.
- No product logo or visual metaphor until the product design is chosen.
- Use boxes, rules and arrows only to expose sequence or evidence relationships.
- Put sources and caveats in small but readable footer text.
- Keep all text and shapes native and editable.
- No animation in the content skeleton. Motion is a later product-led decision.
- Do not use the empty visual system as a style reference for the final deck. The final brand must come from the selected product.

## Speaker rule

- One lead voice owns the story.
- Other teammates control the demo and answer the areas they own in Q&A.
- Notes contain prompts, transitions, timing and caveats, not a memorised script.
- Humour is permitted only when it shortens or clarifies an explanation.

## Pitch judge gate

**Verdict: Proceed with conditions.** Proceed with the brand-free content skeleton, source library, appendix and Figma Slides plumbing. Do not lock the final product story or styled slides.

| Judging lens | Effect | Current basis | Blocking condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brief fit | Strengthens | The sequence makes the unwritten rule and the changed social contract explicit. | Confirm the official wording and show which brief verb the final experience fulfils. |
| Problem identification | Strengthens conditionally | The ordinary-day moment and sender/receiver tension are specific enough to investigate. | A real audience, recurring situation, enforcement signal and consequence still need local evidence. |
| Solution approach | Unknown | The structure reserves a complete sender-to-receiver flow. | No medium or mechanism has beaten chat, paper or a non-digital control. |
| Design innovation | Unknown | Receiver agency and safe closure could become a distinctive mechanism. | A multimedia scrapbook alone is familiar and insufficient. |
| Visual communication | Neutral | The black-and-white skeleton keeps the argument visible without pre-empting product design. | The final rendered deck still needs a product-specific visual review. |
| Presentation skills | Strengthens | The spine uses one human moment, a visible comparison, one demo and a callback instead of a process diary. | Rehearsal must confirm six-minute comprehension and transitions. |
| Evidence quality | Weakest | Relevant external studies exist, but no project test is complete. | No result, quote or product claim may be invented or inferred from audience reaction. |

### Required challenge conditions

- **Assumption and inversion:** receiving must be allowed to complete the interaction without reciprocity.
- **Behaviour and context:** specify where, when and between whom the problem recurs.
- **Medium necessity:** digital must beat or complement ordinary messaging, a physical control and a non-digital ritual.
- **Alternatives:** keep existing channels as credible controls, not straw men.
- **Human specificity:** do not generalise from the team or stereotype men.
- **Inclusion and accessibility:** support reduced motion, readable text, screen readers and a no-device pitch route.
- **Trust, safety and privacy:** show consent, boundaries, deletion and unwanted-contact handling.
- **Failure and recovery:** include misreading, wrong recipient, broken link and safe refusal.
- **Feasibility and demo truth:** label working, simulated and proposed behaviour plainly.
- **Incentives and second-order effects:** avoid reminders, streaks, effort scores and reply pressure until tested.
- **Simplicity and retellability:** the final concept must fit one sentence without listing features.
- **Authorship and distinctiveness:** intimate content remains human-authored; AI use and third-party material are disclosed.

- **Weakest angle:** evidence quality.
- **Main trade-off:** starting the structure now saves time, but attractive placeholders could create false confidence; the brand-free, blocked-state approach prevents that.
- **Next evidence:** a paired sender/receiver comparison using the same message across chat, a physical or crafted control and a rough digital artefact.
